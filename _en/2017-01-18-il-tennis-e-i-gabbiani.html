«If you look up, over there, you can see it well. Further on. There»

«Why do they all go onto that building together?»

«It is Cirillo’s magic. He always brings food and then… I don’t know, they follow him home».

«Are they waiting for him now?».

They were approaching on foot, at a brisk pace. Bags on their shoulders: the child a small racket in its cover, the dad a big holdall. The questions had their usual rapid rhythm and the view was the main source of inspiration, for the one as for the other. How deep is the sea, how many grains of sand are there, how fast is the wind, how many fish do seagulls eat, how high are the waves, where do they come from and… and the clouds, where are the clouds?
It was a clear day, but the sea and the sky merged at the horizon. By the shore the colour was intense and shaded off towards a slightly grey blue that, towards the north, took on new vigour, letting Cirillo’s seagulls draw their swift trajectories against the blue. All together in an unusual dance, happy and noisy.
There was not much left, not much road and not much time before the start of the hour. A few little runs had broken up the march, leaving both short of breath as they talked about the sea, the sand, the tides and… tennis. Tennis, which gently lets you feel the caresses of the ball and moves heart and brain together in search of a harmony that cannot be described. «It never ends, tennis».
It had come out like that, a sentence incomprehensible to a child like his son, but the little one had smiled at once. He had truly lit up with a beautiful joy, as if he were suddenly sure that this day would never end. He trotted, hopped and waved the racket still in its case, gripping it with two hands like a club or a great Viking sword. He smiled and took his left arm, while with the other hand he guided a harmonious movement, gently pushing his body forward: «There, see? Like that. Forward and then, go! Over your head. Ouch! Not like that…». Nothing doing, he wanted to do it by himself, flat out straight away, with all his strength.

The court was already ready, spick and span for the first hour of the morning. There were the balls, cool water and a few chocolates «if you fancy, coffee ones. Not right for the little one, I’ll see if I have a sweet. He can have a sweet, can’t he?”».
Off with the tracksuit bottoms, off with the sweatshirt and a full tank of saintly patience: let us begin to learn. «Hold it like this, move further forward, very good, again. Perfect». An hour in the open air, almost without playing, but the red clay under their feet and a few soft balls hit by chance give a taste of it, to the dad and to the child.
«You give him the best present if you teach him to play».
«And you, Cirillo: don’t you give lessons any more?».
The smile makes his eyes shine and the stories begin, all precise and exactly right for what he has seen, for what the child would need. And the dad too. Was he not there? Was he out of sight? He was going round and pottering, tidying the shed and taking a little bread to the seagulls. And he saw everything, heard everything. He breathed with his court.

____

«30-40»
Another double fault and a twinge in the back. The low light does not help the return; better to keep it in court than to risk the first serve again. Five minutes to go; however it ends, it has not been a great match. A heap of errors, short rallies and one twinge after another. But the backhand was going really well, the cross-court forehand too. A pity about the down-the-line shots, all mistimed, thrown away one after another.
«Too much haste, today we’re really not there».
«You hit some really lovely backhands».
That smile again, which when pressed becomes a story. Never a piece of advice, always a story that in the end… or rather, not in the end - it is hard to say… becomes a story that fits, and from there tennis gets under your skin, a little bit yours, but uncatchable. Beautiful. The court takes on light, the lines redraw themselves, the position and tension of the body become a harmony. You see him play again; you have never seen him, but you feel the ball in your hands as if the racket were not there. A caress, a firm one, accompanies it to where it has to go, on that soft, warm court. Even in winter.
A smile again and a goodbye, until next time, near the seagulls.

____

Tennis never ends, not even in the street, on TV, against a wall, under a damp, cold air dome. It certainly does not end near the sea, spied on by the waiting seagulls, caressed by the wind and the smell of salt. There it begins again, because it is a special place where its magic enters the veins and changes their colour, new and warmer every time.

Tennis never ends because it is like flying; you enter a space bigger than the body and you feel the court the ball the line. The seagulls are there and they watch you; they too learn to fly from that bright, gentle smile. A smile in love.

Tennis is a magic and few people know it; it is like flying, and you watch it from the sky.
This is the secret. Of Cirillo and his seagulls…
