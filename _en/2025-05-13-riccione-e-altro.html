I have not seen the Riccione advert on Mediaset, but the reaction is wrong in every scenario if the alternative idea is to show everything. We could go back to the primary school of marketing, in short. Or rather, perhaps we need to go there for the first time. The ABC that seems to be missing is the distinction between denotation and connotation: getting noticed, standing out, making yourself identifiable, first, and only afterwards going deep, getting into the details, specifying, telling. On a diachronic plane these are moments to keep apart, carefully, for a million reasons.

But it is a trap we fall into every time, in tourism communication: we try to show everything, we try to give a complete panorama by lining up an endless procession of “ands”. And nice things come out, full of enthusiasm and colour, able to convey energy, vitality. But in the long run nothing emerges from the noise: a glass of wine and a sunset, a smile and a romantic embrace, a swimming pool and a little waterfall… you could probably line those up in Cinisello Balsamo or in Kabul too. Piadina included.

And the local produce? And the hinterland? And the works of art? And the Notte Rosa? And the fun, the healthy kind? And the families? And the trekking, the cyclists and the sport? And enough, perhaps.

Yes, it is the “bulirone” we know, the merry hubbub we have always splashed about in, and which has many good things about it. It has worked for almost longer than anyone can remember: why throw it away? Well, in the long run something else might be useful too.

Which is to say that, if someone tries to launch branding initiatives, if they begin a piece of work on image positioning and medium-to-long-term strategy, before hurling anathemas we could go to the primary school of marketing and ask ourselves some questions about the values we are able to express, about the uniqueness we can offer, about identity. It would help a great deal, at every level, from the set-up of services to the building of a shared vision. Because yes, after all, if you have inherited a shop right in the centre, it could be the location that gets you the results and not your skill as a salesman. A legitimate suspicion?
