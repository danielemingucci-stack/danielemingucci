Crisis is the inevitable companion of every epochal passage. It is the fault of the future, which ploughs up the present and in doing so breaks settled balances and undermines stability (and leaderships). That is how it is, and when it happens it happens. The only antidote is to lift our heads and find a different way of looking at things: the energy spent on stopping the mountain is all wasted.

Electric is not selling because it costs too much. Not true. Lined up one after another, the costs of electric are already much lower than combustion. Maintenance and charging are already enough, then there are the knock-on effects on health, the particulates not emitted, the performance and the lower energy required… a whole train of advantages. Which the carmakers still do not see. They are trying to put water into the same jute sack they used to put sand in. And the water runs away.

The ones cheating electric are the energy distributors. If electricity cost less, electric cars would be an obvious advantage for everyone. But today a PUN (the single national price) of €0.10/kWh corresponds to a cost that starts at €0.65/kWh and reaches €0.99/kWh at the charging point. In 2023, with the PUN at €0.20/kWh, you could find tariffs at €0.28/kWh. It is clear they are speculating. So today electric cars are fine only for those who have a garage and perhaps solar panels. For everyone else… worse than shooting yourself in the foot.

Well, I think the answer is obvious: under these conditions demand is being artificially reduced. The carmakers have to rethink their business, reorganise it. And begin supplying low-cost energy to users. Perhaps to do so they could convert the network of maintenance workshops, which will inevitably be scaled down…

There, the road is precisely to stop carrying the old bandwagon around (maintenance, above all) and to direct energy at what really generates a market: build the charging points, distribute the energy needed to move your vehicles, and you will see that the market responds in a different way.
