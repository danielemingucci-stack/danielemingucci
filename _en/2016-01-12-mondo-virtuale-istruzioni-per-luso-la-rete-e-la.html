My children’s school is proposing a meeting with Luigi Ballerini, doctor and writer. Title: “The challenge with the virtual is won with the real. Bringing up children in the age of WhatsApp”

I have a few questions and some ideas. Many prejudices.
An extract from the letter I sent to the head teacher.

“
I have a question about the title: why “virtual”? I feel it as a definition that really means: “it is a world I do not want to exist”. A world that “could” (and that, a little, threatens me). I point it out because for me, instead, WhatsApp (and company) is not a virtual world: it exists and it is a tool for relationships. If there are relationships, we are in the human, and the only possible dynamic is deciding to be there or not to be there. For example, I often hear people distance themselves by saying: “I’m not technological”. Nor am I: if I were, I would know how to build a mobile phone or at least understand how data transmission works. Using WhatsApp (or running a profile on Facebook, Twitter, Tumblr, Instagram, Medium, Google+) is not being technological, it is communicating. It is like talking, and it is not virtual: it is real.

It is so real that our children are there. There too: where are we? Why are we elsewhere? Why are we afraid of losing them? Do we perhaps fear that our reality is less attractive than their “virtuality”? Well, if so, why do we not go with them? Why have we not gone yet? What are we waiting for? Why do we not let ourselves be educated a little, instead of setting ourselves up only, and always, as educators?

Starting from this, I have a provocation and a question: why can my son not use Google at school? Is there any situation in life in which he will not be able to in his future? Why do we not teach him to use it instead of to do without? Checking the credibility of a source (if we are talking about Google) or establishing a relationship (if we are talking about WhatsApp) have, I believe, similar dynamics, whatever the medium through which the action takes place. Looking something up on Google is like looking it up in a library (only much faster); chatting on WhatsApp is like chatting (only much slower). I believe media are places, and that these places are always and in any case inhabited by people. And people, well… that is where the adventure is, is it not?

So, I come to the meeting a little prejudiced, but it is surely only the title’s fault. I believe a great opportunity is concealed under this theme, and that it should be seized as quickly as possible. For example, for twenty years university theses have been done by copy and paste, above all the medical and scientific ones. Text-recognition programs have developed impressively: now, with the web and smartphones, you do not even need a scanner. The school system and the university system go on pretending not to know (the university is in fact evolving; the younger professors do a bit of googling to find the presumed sources of essays, but then assessment becomes measuring how much the student has copied).
But the new technologies can do more and better. They are tools for relationships and they are accelerators of those relationships. They change the dynamics of human relationships only very relatively, in reality, and the only defects they have emerge only when asymmetries are created, that is, when one of the two parties wants to pretend they do not exist. Otherwise, if we admitted their existence and let them into our lives not only as entertainers, the potential they can express is that of accelerating everything. They accelerate, and so they allow us to go further.
An example. Language is a synthesis. “What is your name?”, “Daniele”: what does that say about me? Almost nothing. But to “use” me in a conversation it is all that is needed: “Who made that stupid remark?”, “Daniele”. Ah! That explains everything! Daniele, the one who… and so on. The synthesis of that word accelerates the path of understanding, and our brain, and our heart, is capable of handling this speed, which is indispensable to it for getting further ahead, further on.
Thinking about school, about the training of young people, about education, I believe there are various moments in play, and that there is a part of listening (of study, because when they were aware of what it meant to be able to go to school, students ardently desired to go) and one of production, of creation, of construction. The new media, on this level, bring us a great wealth: being able to be faster allows us to imagine the productive part not only as a check on the learning of notions, but as a moment of exercise in style. If we agree to accelerate, we can concern ourselves with style! Style, personality, character, identity.
If we accept the reality of these new media, if we stop considering this world “virtual”, if we set out to understand how the dynamic of relationship remains in any case between two subjects… well, I believe there is a great deal to discover.
A practical suggestion? Split the computing lesson in half: half an hour of free iPad and half an hour on the beach playing: let us allow ourselves to be educated by the kids, by how they use them, by how they talk about them, by how they learn the new media and explain them to one another (after all, what they learn today about word processors and operating systems will be archaeology in ten years).

”
