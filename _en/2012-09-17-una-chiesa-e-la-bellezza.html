Today I posted a photo: “the most beautiful church in the world”. It is the church where I got married, which has behind it the green of the hill of Corzano, with the sanctuary on top, and in front of it Mount Comero… It really is very beautiful, in an early twentieth-century manner that is, as it is, free from duties of style. A beautiful, beautiful, beautiful church: the most beautiful, I confirm.
Not much time passes and a piqued reprimand reaches me from someone in a big town nearby: I tagged it SanPiero, and heaven forbid! So I needle him a little, provoking: “you are from Cesena, there is a cultural gap that prevents you from understanding”. All hell let loose? If only! “I am not to be given lessons in the Beautiful” and so on, his reply.
Well, answering was easy, because Cesena is an excellent prototype of provincialism (they all dress the same, go on holiday to the same place, and above all always agree with one another), but the real question exploded in my heart…
Lessons are not given in the beautiful and in beauty, because the beautiful and beauty are a wonder that appears before you unforeseen. WONDER, that is the point. There are those who are ready to let themselves be struck and those who, on the contrary, measure and evaluate. Judge. Draw up rankings, weighing things. And - forgive my explicitness - never enjoy (beauty, heaven forbid). “Judgement against wonder”, that wonder which, perhaps for only a second, makes you feel at the centre of the world and in the most beautiful place in the world. Wonder against that totalitarian judgement which does not let itself be so much as brushed by any question (“why would he have said that?”) and at once has to put things back in place, in the ranking.
Wonder against judgement: a total, radical war, with no quarter… Those who arrange their little things in “their” place against those who live (or at least desire, are ready for) infinite horizons.

Once Pigi B., before the splendid Fortress of Bertinoro, said to me: “our civilisation is like this Fortress”… and it really is beautiful, I thought at once … then he turned sharply towards the landscape you command from there and: “but there is one thing that is like this infinite horizon”.

I am beginning to understand the difference…
