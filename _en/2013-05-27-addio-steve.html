Every now and then it pays to listen to the mad. Those who in these days are crying out in despair: “What will become of us without the genius of Steve Jobs”. Today theirs is the most realistic view of reality.

Someone has written that Steve Jobs was not a genius; someone has filed him among those who made their fortune thanks to an idea. I do not know; I believe neither that he was a genius, nor that it was just one idea (or a series of ideas) that made his fortune. I believe he was an extraordinary personality, and that in these last years he obtained what he wanted: he was entrusted with responsibility for the collective vision. What is right and what is wrong… no, that is perhaps too much: what works and what does not work.

As a Mac user from the very first hour, I have had no small amount of fun in these last years watching all the fiercest admirers of PCs collapse and convert one by one: almost as if I were seeing Rosy Bindi cheering for Berlusconi, or the reverse! Jobs sweated for this mass conversion through a series of moves that were all right, one after another. I will not analyse them: the key step was certainly that of the Apps, which shifted a whole generation of programmers from “Dos/Linux/whatever” to the production and programming of Apps. But the sum of this “victory” was a collective entrusting of ourselves, with no parachute and no debate, to the vision of this extraordinary man: the iPhone works even when it does not work, people have been sending MMS since the iPhone stopped being unable to send them, the newspaper never read and certainly never bought now becomes interesting on the iPad, and so on. And on the side of the virtual competitors a single prospect: trying to realise Jobs’s ideas and vision “better” (tablets, touch screens and so on and so forth): no daring to propose alternative roads, but everyone aping or chasing the products of the Apple…

And so on and so on and so on.

In short, what happened is that an imposing mass of people entrusted itself to Steve Jobs’s vision: it felt safe, cool, ahead, in step with the times, fashionable, efficient, creative, because Steve Jobs put a series of tools “in its hands”. I take no exception to the quality of any of them (I have used almost all of them for over twenty years), but I cannot help sensing that the nature of this unconditional adherence shows all the features of a prejudicial passion: it is reassuring to do exactly what a genius recognised by everyone suggests you do; it is reassuring to shut yourself inside the enclosure where the genius hosts you.

Now this genius is no longer here: once the wake of the products he invented has run out and, further on, that of those they will sell us as his ideas or his intuitions, what will become of us? Who will make us feel once again among the “right” ones?

Every now and then it pays to listen to the mad, or rather to look at them: if we look at them now and compare ourselves with them, perhaps we will manage to take back our creativity and our imagination. We will manage once again to sense that one is not free inside an enclosure, not even a gilded one or one full of “options”. We will be able to notice, today, that a tool is a tool, but that the genius is whoever puts it to work: whoever uses it to build. And here, dear all, the vision and the imagination have to come from us.

The times ahead will give us great opportunities to put to use the product of Steve Jobs’s labour. As protagonists.

Daniele Mingucci

8.10.2011
