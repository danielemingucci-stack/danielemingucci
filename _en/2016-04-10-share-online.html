The video of a friend who passed away this morning and a little dog licking the back of the sweetest little girl. A few pearls from Montemagno and a lot of reposts and shares. One or two original photos, and strangely today I am spared the Juventus euphoria (perhaps they have stopped; they too will get bored sooner or later). It was hot air, Facebook; now it is reheated air. I look at my profile and notice that I too, little by little, have let shares and reposts take hold; my own stuff is less and less. Compulsive checking becomes compulsive sharing, but this very word “sharing” is perhaps emptying itself out in a whirling word of mouth that is… yes, a little empty. So empty that sharing the grief for a friend gone for ever lasts just long enough to scroll to the next kitten that forces a smile out of you and distracts you, then a piece of sports news and the friend photographed at the radio station… Goodbye. A child abandoned in a field, a newborn, is safe, and the hilarious cartoon straight below it anaesthetises everything with a laugh. Renzi, as always, everything fine.
I am a fan of sociality, I am a fan of sharing, I like discussing and exchanging opinions and impressions, vigorously too; I am often told off for putting “too much stuff on Facebook”… But the oxymorons are more and more excessive, intrusive, alienating, and one almost begins to suspect that there is a specific algorithm designed to keep an average and defuse feelings. Like the algorithm of Del Debbio or of La Gabbia, to keep the level of complaint and anger at a certain pitch. Something capable of helping me feel the way I ought to feel. But perhaps, after all, at the Coop and at Esselunga too they decide what I should eat by guiding me with special offers…
The medium “is” the message, yes. The medium is the message. A mysterious sentence, easy at first, but then once you go into it, getting lost takes a moment. These lines would have stayed imprisoned in a notebook, for example; now twenty people will read them. Quickly; they will not share them, or they will, more no than yes, but perhaps they will have even less life than shut between dusty pages… “I saw you’ve got a plaster cast”, “yes, I know you came through Milan”, “Yes, I couldn’t make it to Marina’s funeral, but I saw you were all there”… The medium is the message. Everything happened, but it happened on Facebook. On WhatsApp, on Messenger.
Have we really shared anything?
