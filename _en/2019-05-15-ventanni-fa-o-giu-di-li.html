The essence of feudalism always shows itself with great linearity, if you read it as the transfer of a merit. Its base is privilege, and its perpetuation is the display of that privilege to consolidate an acquired position. No subterfuge, no ambiguity. The mechanism is that of inheritance, a mechanism that has been almost natural for millennia, based on law and also on the preservation of the species, in a certain sense.

Society, all societies, bear its deep mark, and it is a form of order certainly to be preserved. If the fumes of youth can make one cry out against castes, later, when children are involved, it is easy to come to compromises, to accept shortcuts. It is everyone’s sin; nobody has the right to judge.

The trigger, though, is the point at which the betrayal is revealed, at least in situations where - and here, instead, someone with a clean conscience can still be found - it goes beyond the bond of blood. Transfer of merit: one takes the position, the inheritance of someone who earned it on merit and then had to leave it, through death, obviously. Legitimate, but that merit has to be earned again. That is, eyes must be kept on the merit, not on the position. Gazes must be concentrated on the essence of that merit, not on the effect, on conservation.

They are marking twenty years since the death of a man, a great man beside whom it was a real privilege to be able to stand; it was beautiful, simply beautiful. Intensity, truth, a challenge to reality, closeness to all the absolutes that make life great. I want from his heirs the same sensations, the same presence, the same liveliness. I want to be looked at in the same way; I cannot be content with evocations, with facts narrated: they are worth as much as séances in which the only value is the acquired “privilege” of whoever has the right to summon the spirit. I want the spirit, not the summoning.

But it is not a protest, it is simply a trial; it is not a test, it is a lively request: whoever puts themselves forward to continue a road must sooner or later take care of the journey, not of the order in which people walk. The commander who won the fief put his heart into what he did, looked his soldiers, his travelling companions, in the eye one by one. The heir cannot stop at haranguing the old warriors: our heart beats for the battle, not for a trench or a castle.
