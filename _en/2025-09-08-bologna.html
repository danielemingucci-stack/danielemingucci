Always.

It happens here, certain things happen only here. It is the land, not the people. Of course it is the people, but the land comes first, somehow. Italy is like that too, in general: anyone who stops for a while becomes Italian. But for Italy it is a way of welcoming, of living together. Bologna, instead, is a mess. Not in that sense, and not in the other one either. But it always happens here. It happens here that the university, that civil rights, that the people, the organisations… all below the surface, with those brusque and welcoming ways, but always here, one way or another. The porticoes, the hills, the inns, the women, the men, the singers and the poets. And the organisations. The cursing, few blasphemies, perhaps none, but not out of respect, not out of fear. The square, a big one, San Petronio and San Luca. The students, the mopeds, the junkies and the drunks. The cold, the political factions, the osterias and the tarot cards. The little shops, the copy shops, more osterias, the pastry-and-cappuccino bars. The pigeons, under the wheels and on the crossbeams of the porticoes, Neptune’s straight cock and the palace of King Enzo. The Archiginnasio (is the capital all right?) and so on and so forth. Oh! The Giardini Margherita and the hospitals. Bologna. Where it is not only the big ones who speak, where it is not only the strong who command, where the rich do not go first. Because they mix in very well, the rich, in the queue and in the traffic, under the porticoes and on the pavé.

It happens here, it all happens here. From here threads unwind and branch out everywhere, in an unpredictable and absent-minded way, uncommitted and democratic. Which is a concept that has to be associated with food, but then a world opens up in which I am no expert. The wine, the bread, the mortadella. But also the little pizzas and a jumble of cured meats that would need a census, a Liber Paradisus of their own, if the parallel is not blasphemous. The ragùs, the tagliatelle and the neighbours’ things, from Modena or from Romagna. Democracy, at the table and under the porticoes. Sharing, parity, egalitarianism. I am talking at random, but freedom here is different. More complex, in some ways, but wider. There is balance, coexistence, parity. Which is a way of saying listening, pissed-off listening, polemical, aggressive. But listening, relation, contact. A kairòs out of place, fat and desperate, perhaps, but active. Alive.

Bologna is the most beautiful place in the world, somehow, but it is an unconfessable secret, an obscene one. Because it is so cold you could die and so hot you could suffocate. Because everything works when the moment comes, but you cannot see it and it all looks haphazard. It is socmèl, in everyone’s mouth, and it is poetry in the colour of the sky, next to that of the roofs and the houses and the cut-off towers. Yes, somehow, it is the most beautiful place in the world.
