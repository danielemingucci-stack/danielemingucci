I do many things, it is true, but I do not think they are too many. A cross-contamination of contexts, of languages, of knowledge, to which - almost inexorably - encounters attach themselves that are always extraordinary, along with stimuli that become real invitations to put oneself on the line: I live every idea that is born in this spirit, and I marvel at how not backing out, accepting to play at several tables, multiplies energy and suggestion, widens horizons and possibilities.

Yes, it is an excusatio non petita, but it is right that I should ask myself whether those who urge me not to do too many things, so as not to do them all badly, have a point… I justify myself by saying that the days are very long and the nights even longer: there is an infinite space in which to let oneself be carried by dreams and drawn in by challenges. In short, a novel of mine is coming out, short, mean, experimental, written for fun around the time of Covid and polished at leisure until Amedeo Bartolini of NFC told me: «I’ll publish it for you».

Wow.

Written for fun, yes, at the start. But not all that much, because - in a strange and irreverent way - it talks about love, which is the most important subject I know and, I have to admit, the thing that interests me most.

It is called “L’amore può attendere”, Love Can Wait, which is a great lie.

L'amore può attendere - Daniele Mingucci
