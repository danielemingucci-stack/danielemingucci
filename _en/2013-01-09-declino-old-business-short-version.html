The turnovers of old companies are falling vertiginously, but it is not the fault of the crisis. On the contrary: the crisis depends on them too. Zero regard for design, zero regard for new technologies, zero regard for the new sales channels and the new strategies of relationship with users. Everything staked on the traditional network of agents and on sales margins. The only levers of action are cutting costs and the consequent cuts in employees and collaborators.

In lean times value systems emerge, and these are often based on ephemera: “clothes make the man” or “there are no friends at work”…
What emerges is a stubborn short-sightedness towards the potential values that our labour market, our craftsmanship, the envied creativity of the Italian humus is able to produce. The company diktats are very clear: costs must be low, innovation is only a problem, design is an accessory. And, in communication, creativity and new languages are senseless taboos.

Innovation is the real victim. We stay one or even two steps behind the Chinese. It is they who innovate, do research, produce. The steady drip of semi-finished products bought below cost in China causes a haemorrhage of money that will not come back into circulation in Europe for a long time and, now, the capacity for research, innovation and invention on the part of our human substrate is being emptied out too…
The banks, unfortunately, cultivate this type of entrepreneur, offering them those channels of access to credit that are instead locked tight for young startuppers or innovative projects. The most disturbing aspect of the whole affair is that this establishment of entrepreneurs and credit institutions has created an unassailable monolith that is literally depressing every single personality and every single personal creativity through company strategies and “morals” that are despairing and effective only in the very short term. In the company nobody dreams, no great projects are realised, nothing is truly built. The aim is immediate profits, the aim is a briefly glittering success, materialised only in an image “of success” that is more and more out of tune in these times. The human and creative potential, personal and intimate, of the people involved in the work is mutilated of its best parts. Employees are, in a certain sense, constantly swindled by company policies imposed by an authority based on the trailing remains of dominant positions by now out of time.
Today the blame for the crisis is laid entirely on the politicians, and they defend themselves by laying everything on the “tax evaders”. I believe instead that the most significant share of responsibility is lodged in the everyday mentality with which business is done and with which people work. In the ideal and operational horizon within which one encloses one’s professional activity. At whatever level, but the fish stinks from the head, and the head is not the politicians. The head is those who manage the budgets for development and growth (or consolidation) of companies. And it is those who activate or keep “dormant” the new channels of relationship with the global market. In case anyone still did not know, in fact, quality Made in Italy sells around the world. And sells a lot. Let us put this question to entrepreneurs and marketing managers: how much are you risking? On what? To whom will you answer?
