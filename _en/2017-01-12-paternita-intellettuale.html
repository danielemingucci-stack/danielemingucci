The sharing economy seems not to exist in reality, except in connection with the creation of platforms - proprietary ones - which in the best of cases manage to group interests or bring stakeholders together. What gets shared, though, is paradoxically the “differences” and not the points in common. Let me explain: groups of non-competitors are created, characterised by complementary skills, but there is no real possibility of working, together, towards a goal and towards the creation of an ecosystem except by giving away, giving up, putting off, or even delegating the profit to others. No sharing, on this level. (The classic American-film pattern is that of a thing born in a group, and then one of them, at a certain point, puts everything in his own name and cashes in, leaving the crumbs to his ex-friends).

The possibility of creating a virtuous circle in which it pays to work together rather than against, or simply for oneself, is at present a utopia that is disappointing those who believed in it: the myth of Silicon Valley has created mega-companies just like those people wanted to bring down in the ’70s/’80s. At the moment the only level on which to move is that of price: creating a kind of solution that has to be worth it, to simplify, to speed up the process and to put at the disposal of the market - or of the players in the market - technology, quality, performance at a price at which it pays them to “use” you rather than compete with you or copy you.

If we could act on the level of law, instead, we ought to move from the concept of Intellectual Property to that of Intellectual Paternity. A nuance, perhaps, but one that replaces the concept of exclusivity with that of participation. The intellectual owner forbids or grants use; the “father” benefits from it, even beyond his own will (he cannot forbid the use, but something is owed to him).
I do not know how this dynamic could be set off, but I believe it could greatly accelerate the performance of innovation, both by creating a climate of competitive calm (everyone works towards a common goal) and by taking time and resources away from defensive activities, which are very costly. It would be advantageous for the system, in short, as well as rewarding for the specialisation of skills: those who know how to invent, invent; those who know how to market, market…
