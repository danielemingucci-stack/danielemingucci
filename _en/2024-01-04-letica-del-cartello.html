When prices all go up together, you cannot help suspecting there is something shady underneath: they have formed a cartel! Bingo! But I am beginning to think that is not always true. And it is not good news, because the impression is that there is a kind of stagecoach hold-up in which the “price makers” (can we call them that?) reason with a very precise logic: how much money do you have in your pocket? Right… give me that. At that point the price is set by the market - and these are the basics - and it is completely detached from the cost of the product. At BricoSomething, sandpaper costs €9; at the ironmonger’s a metre of it comes to less than two euros (after all, what do accountants and white collars improvising at DIY know? The money in their pocket they have, the time to stop by the ironmonger’s they have not). The electricity companies that produce green energy charge for it like those that produce it from oil, gas and coal, making stellar profits, and so on. How much money do we have? Right… that will do, there is no other thought.

This also means we will never pay a penny less than what we used to pay. Why? Because if you were paying it before, it means you have it, so I, who set the price, come and take it, and the margin I keep for myself. I have endless examples, but so does everybody.

Fine, business is business, money is money and so on and so forth. But with this logic we can forget about growing, forget about anything changing, anything at all. It is a logic of mutual exploitation that does not allow any talent or any idea to really break free. Someone looks you in the eye and simply “tracks” you (it is the salesman’s cookie gaze :) with the sole aim of squeezing the most out of your wallet. I, on the other side, stop trusting, and so on.

Is there a way out? Perhaps politics could do something: is tracking easy today? Shall we track the paths by which a price is built? Shall we set “ethical” limits, as with interest rates under anti-usury logic: is it not the same thing? It would be an advantage in the opposite direction too, one that goes from high pricing to recognising value to producers, for example.

Yes, ethics by law is never ideal, but something has to be done… otherwise the ethics of the cartel prevails, meaning there is not even any need to form the cartel in order to be in agreement.
