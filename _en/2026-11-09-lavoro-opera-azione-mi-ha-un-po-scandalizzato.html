Arendt rather scandalised me with how little regard she gives to Labour in the system set out in The Human Condition, her Vita Activa. Mere sustenance, something that burns itself up in a circle until it is almost worth nothing, when in fact it holds all of life inside it, and the smiles, and the wonder of days following one another. And that Work, so concrete, so full of itself that it thinks it can really outlast us, almost defying time, the eternal. And then Action, which at first I misunderstood.

It is, instead, a realistic progression, and in many ways an exhilarating one. Because first comes necessity, then comes the measuring of oneself against time (individual, instrumental in relationships, for the most part) and finally come what she calls “actions”. Relations, relationships, community, the agon. Present and presence, a true “being-there” (who knows whether I am quoting him properly, Heidegger).

On the two legs of labour and work - which depend on everything, on conditions, on possibilities, on talent - the path of action begins, in relation, in confrontation and comparison with those around us. Relation, and the sharing of goals, visions, research and discovery. A shared path. Comparison and clash, perhaps, but still a relation that truly puts the human to the test and gives a horizon to what we do.

It is no accident, I now understand, that the activa of the title refers to vita. In action, in this web of relations, comparisons, compromises, challenges and so on, is contained life itself, the living kind.

So, above the constraint of talents, conditions and possibilities, new contexts of calling and of calls are built (challenges, provocations, tangles, suggestions, inspirations or complications) that show themselves “man to man”, as they say, person to person. Challenges, calls (let us take one of the words from the list above) that bring people into relation and call them to trust one another and to generate relationships that will then have to be built up (and so designed and constructed), set to work, put to the test. And these will be the human path of a life, the thing that makes it something other than a list of things, other than a quantitative display of results, carrying it instead to a level of real relation with the search for meaning, and of relation with sense. Human.
