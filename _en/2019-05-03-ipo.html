Hypocrisy is built by working on the minuses, not on the pluses. It is not a covering up, an exaggerating: it is hiding, concealing. You adjust, taking away as when you sculpt. And you create a form suited to the comedy to be played. What gets hidden, first of all, are the real expectations. You come to terms with yourself and build a strategy. It is an instinctive process, not a real strategy (nobody “wants” to be a hypocrite): you follow the reasonings one after another, starting from the premise that has been fixed. A kind of great amplification, a multiplication that begins with a rigged factor (a multiplicand). Hypocrisy has a stringent rationality and creates enormous friction with everyone, because the lie is so far back that, if you want to flush it out, you really have to dig deep. For this reason the hypocrite is almost invincible and almost certainly lost at the outset. He has no doubts, no uncertainties, no blank patches. The reasonings, the causal links, roll like stones in a landslide. It is incredible how a single minus can carry hundreds of pluses with it. How it can beat them all together, with a single blow, even a badly aimed one.

The trick of sincerity is the only one that can be applied. Should it cost a clash, should it cost a blank patch, even a serious one, it deserves the sacrifice. Sincerity with oneself, with one’s own unease, with what one feels is missing. It is a beginning, a small one, but at least a beginning different from the one that buries life in a hypocritical comedy. And an insincere one.
