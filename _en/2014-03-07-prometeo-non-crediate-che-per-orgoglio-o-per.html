PROMETHEUS: Do not think that out of pride or out of arrogance I keep silent: I gnaw at my heart to see myself like this. But hear the sorrows of mortals; and how, witless as they were, I made them intelligent and endowed with sense. Once, they looked and did not see, they heard and did not understand: and like the shapes of dreams, throughout their long life they confused everything at random. And they knew no sunny houses of brick, nor the art of working wood: but they dug themselves dens under the ground, like nimble ants, in the depths of sunless caves. Nor had they any sure sign of winter, or of flowery spring, or of fruitful summer, until I taught them to discern the risings and the uncertain settings of the stars. And number too, supreme among inventions, I found for them; and the varied composition of letters, memory of all things, industrious mother of every culture. And I was the first to tame the animals, so that they might take the place of mortals in the hardest labours. And no one else, if not I, invented the hulls that, wandering over the sea on wings of linen, carry seafarers. And I, who found such inventions for men, I myself now, wretched as I am, have no device by which to escape this torture.

Aeschylus
