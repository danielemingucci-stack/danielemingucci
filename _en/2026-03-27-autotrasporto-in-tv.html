Canale 5, morning, between eight and half past. A space of compassion and listening for the stricken haulier (he has some fifty articulated lorries, but hardship strikes without looking anyone in the face): with these prices, with this crisis, with this war he will have to raise his rates. And no, people do not realise: it is not only at the pump that we pay a price. We pay the price a thousand times over, on every item. And he will raise his rates. He would rather not, but he is forced to. He will not lower them when the crisis is over, but meanwhile let us think of the present and immerse ourselves in this crisis, all the way down. We listen to him stammer, stunned; we watch him as he turns around, disoriented. We breathe his every sigh, we stumble together with him over every signal delay, when the journalist’s question arrived late. It is a climax that takes the words away, that fills even the silences with tension. At this rate, where will we end up? It is a question that hovers in every breath, immersed in this present so absurd that «paradoxically» (it is he, the haulier, who says it!) electric mobility now pays. Yes, he has diesel lorries, LNG ones and electric ones, and this crisis has led to the most extreme paradox: electric that pays!

Well, let us go into it - I think to myself - into this paradox. Nothing doing. They do not even say goodbye to him; he barely finishes the sentence.

It all ends in an instant, without even time for a thank you or a have a nice day. The paradox must stay there, like a gaffe, in the absurd green mania for this technology that is 95% efficient instead of the 40% of diesel engines and, inexplicably, even costs less.

Lobby, lobby on the wall, who are the biggest charlatans of them all?
