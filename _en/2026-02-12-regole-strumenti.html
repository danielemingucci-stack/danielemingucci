Building equality is not all being the same; it is offering each person an acceptable range of possibilities, freedom. The instrument of construction is rules and laws, following an approach that is on one side political (regulating relations with balance and vision) and on the other technical (defining processes with precision). Along this road we arrive at setting acceptable standards, which are certainly step one in the creation of equality. What language is spoken (in the broad sense too), on what parameters we measure ourselves, within what perimeter we move, which relations must be considered. And so on. Rules that, as in a checklist, define values and separate good from bad.

This is a definition of values by subtraction: with rules, what inevitably prevails is the punishment (the non-acceptance) of the disvalue, rather than the full affirmation of the positive value.

It is a process of selection that becomes a process of choice: choice is the positive face of selection, but it does not differ by much, not by enough. We discard and we choose.

This process is a gratifying one because it is the exercise of a power, of a free will, as we say. Both “free” and “will” define a force of independence and of irreducible uniqueness (singularity?): that is what makes it gratifying. And divisive: the self is affirmed by exclusion. Another gratification: access to an exclusive context.

Skipping a few steps, we arrive at a kind of definition of self through what we choose. We place our own image high up by virtue of exclusive choices based on values, on the acceptance of high standards and so on. Upstream of all this there is - or there should be - the implicit recognition (sic! here is the point) of the values on which those standards are set. In any case, paradoxically, the process is a process of exclusion, of distancing and also of entrenchment. What?! Are we exaggerating? Yes, in a certain sense, and no if we want to look at the dynamic in its naked essentials.

Values laid down through rules do not inspire and do not move us; they put us in front of walls that force us to choose: good or bad.

It is not the only possible reading, though, because those walls are embankments that let the river run faster towards the sea. That is exactly the point: we have to learn to look towards the sea in order to use rules properly and fly towards our destination. Then rules are tools (it is tools that allow humanity to progress, is it not?). Gaining this awareness lets us place rules in a wider scenario, make them lovable and functional, and take from them the “power” of exclusively defining the guiding value. It is not rules that generate value; they serve the enjoyment of that value’s effects. Our whole posture changes, because for example we will stop deluding ourselves that simply following the rules guarantees the affirmation of the value. From this angle, in fact, the value truly lies beyond, past our capacity for control and possession, past our power, and no rule, like the other tools, will be considered enough to define it completely.

The scenario of needs and attitudes set in motion before a value changes too. Inspiration, an open mindset, processes of updating and evolution become essential for conforming to the value and improving our adaptation to it, as we move towards it and gain awareness and skill.

As a corollary, we can also equip ourselves with better criteria for judging the generation of processes and standards based on the flowering of ever new and progressively ambitious rules: are we feeding processes of exclusion, or are we truly inspiring? That is: do we define value by subtraction, or do we open ourselves to discovering it?

A subtlety again, but also a radical change of mindset and posture. And perhaps also a practical help not to wrap ourselves up in a pandemonium of tangles, so as to let ourselves truly be drawn and lit by the key value. Without the illusion of being able to generate it by our own efforts alone.

From generating rules to generating tools, in short, both the practice and the horizon change.
