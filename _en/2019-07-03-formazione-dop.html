Today training, above all at managerial levels, is a trip based on emotion. You live moments in which you feel right, on a par with the greats; you compare yourself with unimaginable success stories; they are taken apart to find a simplified moral and to arrive straight at the secrets (always simple and disruptive in a blindingly obvious way). With this approach, motivation, adrenaline and positive feelings are pushed to the maximum, aptitudes are stimulated by consolidating the perception of having almost infinite room for improvement and of having great leaps forward within reach. It is a con: the greats of history are either the first or the only ones in what they do. And they never paid for a ticket. To set dormant energies in motion, the idea of stimulating direct comparisons with great success stories really does have many merits, but the unforgivable defect is that it drugs you with adrenaline, creates emotional shocks different from the authentic ones, from those of real performance: they are never those, the emotions lived by the “greats” you are being brought alongside. We are in the gym; they were on the road. Not only that: by drugging perceptions, cognitive abilities are altered, and for this reason the enthusiastic circus inhabited by managers and executives could be a sentence rather than a privilege, could have the effect of switching off rather than switching on, of satisfying emotionally rather than setting a change in motion. Of being enough unto itself. Do I mean to insinuate that training has mainly the purpose of perpetuating itself as a market and of increasing profit margins as its first objective? I have no evidence, but it is a pity that the same system is applied to young people, even to children: NBA methods applied at summer camps for pimply players from provincial schools, Serie A-level discipline over kit for the under-eights at football, hackathons for inexperienced high-school students. Efficient, winning methods are introduced, high-performance strategies that put you in a professional’s mood by skipping all the steps: the defect is not trivial, because the perception of reality is falsified, and one proceeds by moving away from one’s actual condition. A distance is cancelled, a helicopter is borrowed and the path is viewed from above: to get here you go through there. Simple, no? Finally, fresh and rested from not having made the journey, the view from above is marvellous. But the truth is that the imbalance between emotion and reality, between perception and situation, does not have the strength to bring you closer to your own improvement: it only postpones the problem. So continuing learning becomes an interminable tour of non-places (or of merely emotional places) in which no talents will grow, and from which the best thing to hold on to is to study carefully those who leave out of unease, hoping that at least among them some talent can be found, a little hunger, some anger, the right unwillingness to pay for a ticket in order to have a seat.Cancelling the gap, removing the teacher’s desk and the disconnection between “subjects” and immediate needs, eliminating the formal and sophisticated perfection demanded by teachers, wiping out the long holiday of a journey far from the destination… I continue to find only merits in classical education, distant and abstract, which has forced so many minds to find links, to change point of view, to accept tortuous paths on which it is better to pass over emotional satisfactions. Today, instead, they make you feel what you are not, they show you destinations without preparing you for the walk, with emotional electroshocks that abandon you exhausted at the start of the path, waiting for the next trip, always far from the peaks. Long live pussy, in short, written in secret in your diary during the Greek lesson.The greats never paid for a ticket; it is time to spend those sums better.
