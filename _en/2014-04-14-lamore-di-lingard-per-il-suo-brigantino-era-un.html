Lingard’s love for his brig was a man’s love, so great that it could be satisfied only when he drove the ship to display all her qualities and her power, to repay his affection with a fidelity tested to the very limit of endurance. Every quiver of the sails came down from aloft along the taut bolt-ropes and poured into his heart a sense of keen happiness; and the gentle murmur of the water along the sides, which, continuous and subdued, showed that in all those turns his incomparable vessel had never lost her way, not even for an instant, was to him more precious and more exalting than tender words whispered in another man’s ear would have been. It was in moments like those that he lived intensely, in a rush of feelings so strong as to make him long to press his little ship to his breast. She was his perfect world, full of trusting joy.

J. Conrad, The Rescue (retranslated from the Italian).
