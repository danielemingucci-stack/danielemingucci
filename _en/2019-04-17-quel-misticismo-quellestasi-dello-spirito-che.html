That mysticism, that ecstasy of the spirit, which reveals itself one last time with so much melancholy and tenderness in Petrarch, was in open rupture with the tendencies and habits of a society that was cultured, erudite, artistic, given over to enjoyments and material cares, still Christian in its intellect, neither sceptical nor materialist, but in its life already indifferent and incurious about the high problems of humanity. The language was the same, but behind the word the thing was no longer there. This was everyone’s secret, that something unnoticed and undefined, which nonetheless showed itself with such clarity in practical life.

F. De Sanctis, History of Italian Literature
