Today there are social media to act as varnish and, around election time, you find yourself with planned micro-invasions in which small masses charge in head down and begin a single-issue activity on the subject of choice (9 times out of 10, the town). I put up photos of the children, the first little tooth and the Zorro costume; on the other side I find a monologue addressed to the prime minister, full of “Your Excellency” and “those who”. A bit of nonsense about hosting Ukrainian women refugees has as its counterpart a profound thought on the Exhibition Centre and the Palas…
The new whitewashed frontier travels on social media and has one unmistakable feature: one-way traffic. Social? The idea of social is that “they” speak and you listen. The most appreciated feature of Facebook? That you can only say like. It is all you have to do, besides commenting “beautiful, brilliant, I support it”.
Ah, these social media, these enormous protagonists of mass stupefaction! On these occasions you appreciate them a little more, because at least they bring out clearly the ability and the inability to create two-way relationships. When you see these bulls in a china shop using Facebook to spread articles and press releases, when you see zero care for language in interminable, concept-heavy gallops, that is when you realise that… oh well, on average they give up 3 or 4 hours after the count begins anyway (some already from the day of electoral silence): that is a good thing, because you do then get a few years of peace.
