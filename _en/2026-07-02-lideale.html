We are trying to inspire our kids responsibly, guided by so many things to do. Commitments as well as duties. Which rationalises everything, since some things are more important than others, some things more useful and some less. Judgement guides us, and one of the declared enemies is urgency, because planning helps us do things well and measure out strength, resources, energy. The things to do.
The dynamic of the ideal, though, is a different one. Urgency, emergency, wonder overwhelm the concern of those who make plans and schedules. There are no things to do, no responsibilities, only urgency, emergency, wonder. It is a whirlpool of wonders - plural - that crosses seasons and routines, letting itself be struck by the cry of the world and by the urgency that everything be saved and set free. That is why I cannot wait, that is why I chafe where people stall, lining up their commitments. The ideal somehow presses, always; it asks for wonder without hesitation or uncertainty, it asks for it now, always. And it deserves it now.

But what is it? The ideal is presence, a relation with meaning here and now. It is vision, utopia, dream, imagination, yes. But above all it is presence. Because these are prerogatives that are triggered and lit before the heart, and that discreetly invade - perhaps with nostalgia and pain - first the gaze and then our actions. Care and compassion, tenderness and frenzy live together, uncertain perhaps, but they move the present.
