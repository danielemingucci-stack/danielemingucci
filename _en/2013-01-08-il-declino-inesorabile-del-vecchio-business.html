A collection of Porsches and a turnover in free fall: this is where you can end up (in the technology sector and not only there).
How? It is not too difficult… A few pointers.
Zero regard for design, zero regard for new technologies, zero regard for the new sales channels and the new strategies of relationship with users. Everything staked on the traditional network of agents and on sales margins. The only levers of action are cutting costs and the consequent cuts in employees and collaborators. Very simple.
“Old” business has probably always been dirty, but now that the times are overtaking it, it is truly offering us the worst from every point of view, the human one included. If, in fact, in the fat years some benevolent likeableness can emerge even from the most grotesque characters, in the lean years, when one has to choose what to remove and what to keep, where to put resources and where to cut, well, this is the moment when value systems come out in style. And sermons and proclamations rain down under the banner of “clothes make the man” or “there are no friends at work” and so on. And the Porsches rain down too, bought and collected “for the good of the company and the employees” (above all the sacked ones, no?), useful for giving an “image of success”. Yes, because if clothes make the man, the Porsche (perhaps a diesel, how sad!) makes the good entrepreneur: as if company register searches did not exist, or the circulation of information about lay-offs and bank exposure…
Fine, but beyond this, what emerges is a stubborn short-sightedness towards the potential values that our labour market, our craftsmanship, the envied creativity of the Italian humus is able to produce. The company diktats are very clear and we have already listed them: costs must be low, innovation is only a problem, design is an accessory… and so on. Over communication (creativity and new languages), then, let us draw a merciful veil.
From the point of view of product innovation, the result is to be one or even two steps behind the Chinese. Yes, two steps behind the Chinese! They innovate, do research, produce. We perhaps assemble, but above all we buy cheap and sell dear “here at home”, with the prospect, already partly realised and devastating, of completely emptying our land: it has been emptied of money thanks to the steady drip of semi-finished products bought below cost in China; now it is being emptied too of the capacity for research, the capacity for innovation and invention on the part of our human substrate…
In this devastating saga an unlikeable part is played, as usual, by the banks, which, with a similar short-sightedness, cultivate this type of entrepreneur, offering them those channels of access to credit that are instead locked tight for young startuppers or innovative projects. The Porsches, again, have their effect, but it is not only that. Old-style entrepreneurs, in fact, are “animals” that are simple for credit institutions to manage: they direct everything towards bricks and mortar or towards more or less hidden personal deposits, so if things turn bad it is easy to recover. But above all they are people who repeat the same pattern serially, so that when their economic and financial means begin to be vast it is extremely simple to involve them in “high finance” projects run by very expensive bank consultants who recommend very expensive consultancy and investments, always with the banks. And so on. They are excellent suckers, in short: the best of all is to convince them to list on the stock exchange (even if today that is a little less fashionable because of the world crisis). In that case the sucker brings out any sum and launches into formidable acquisitions and borrowings, blinded by the prospect of stellar earnings. No problem: everyone gains, given that he will put everything back into circulation with the same pattern and, again, into bricks and mortar (always good as a guarantee)…
As for eating it all up, the children or grandchildren will see to that, but that is another story.
The most disturbing aspect of the whole story, though, is not this game of sharks eating one another and building riches with reckless little games and the management of zero-point-somethings. The truly disturbing aspect is that this establishment of entrepreneurs and credit institutions has created an unassailable monolith that is literally depressing the whole people: every single personality and every single personal creativity is depressed by company diktats that are despairing and effective only in the very short term. In the company nobody dreams, no great projects are realised, nothing is truly built. The aim is immediate profits, the aim is a briefly glittering success, materialised only - am I becoming boring? - in the Porsche of the moment. The human and creative potential, personal and intimate, of the people involved in the work is mutilated of its best parts. Employees are, in a certain sense, constantly swindled by company policies imposed by an authority based solely on the merit of having earned (through work that was certainly not only its own) channels of access to credit closed to others. Yes, because those company or personal Porsches too are bought by taking on more debt on the company’s shoulders.
I have told this story because today the blame for the crisis is laid entirely on the politicians, on this one or that one, it makes little difference, and they defend themselves by laying everything on the “tax evaders”. I believe these things are not wrong, but I believe that most of the responsibility is lodged in the everyday mentality with which business is done and with which people work. In the ideal and operational horizon within which one encloses one’s professional activity. At whatever level, but the fish stinks from the head, and the head is not the politicians. The head is those who manage the budgets for development and growth (or consolidation) of companies. And it is those who activate or keep “dormant” the new channels of relationship with the global market. In case anyone still did not know, in fact, quality Made in Italy sells around the world. And quite a lot.
Let us put this question to entrepreneurs and marketing managers: how much are you risking? On what? To whom will you answer?
