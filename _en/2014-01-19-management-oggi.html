I watched The Apprentice. Yes, I know, it is ridiculous to begin a reflection with “I watched The Apprentice”. And it is ridiculous too to base one on TV, but there it is… I was immediately intimidated by the imposing CVs and by the ostentation of ability put on stage. The blow was even heavier because of the unbearable game of phrases and declarations of intent in eighties/nineties style about success, being the right sort and ambition… That it should be dinosaurs of such a cast who are destined for leadership truly laid me low… Then the game brought everything back to the earth I know and showed the real potential of that drawing-room culture, and the total inability of such an approach to generate business. It is the culture of spending the budget, the culture of phoney brainstorming and of the total inability to make a team and to generate ideas… It is the drama of today, in my opinion. Everything is dumped onto politics and politicians, who, let us be clear, deserve every charge, but the drama does not end there. There is a whole generation of managers used to handling other people’s assets, who build careers by flaunting jargon and double-breasted suits without the slightest ability to make the business grow. Like the aliens in American films: they move from one planet to another to suck up resources and then pass on to the next.
It is a managerial culture identical to that of the politicians, only centred on spending, on the consumption of resources. Producing resources serves to generate resources to spend, but the pivot of the whole matter is always and only “having the budget”, because it is on this budget that they earn.
It was hilarious to see a few of them on TV, masked and fake from the big toe to the tip of the nose. But in the real world there is reason to be afraid when you find them in front of you, meek and know-it-all, deciding - pardon my French - with other people’s arses.
