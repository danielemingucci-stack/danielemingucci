Do you want to get back the urge to use your old windsurf board? Put it up for sale online. Last year I rashly bought a windsurfer: I felt like it, after so many years without one; I sensed that I rather needed some carefree space and could still enjoy myself. But I used it only once. I was already on the beach with the family, the sirocco starts to build in the right way and you can see the whitecaps offshore. An exchange of glances with the blonde and I am off: I stop by the house and in six minutes load everything onto the car, another 25 minutes and I am ready with everything rigged on the shore. Into the water, one foot on, one pump of the sail and away. A few metres, a couple of waves, the board starts to plane, I put my feet in the straps… I am still the man I used to be! And the wind drops. A hundred metres, a little more… I put the boom on the stern, turn the board, get under the sail, lift everything as you are supposed to… no wind, there is not even enough air to hold the sail over my head. On top of that the jacket that is supposed to be a life jacket is a truly unbearable hindrance. Nothing doing, I climb onto the board and take the uphaul (I would rather not even have rigged it!), but between giant sail, jacket and waves it is quicker to get back in the water and bring it all ashore. Swimming. Exactly as in the old days.

It is not for me, in short. Lovely, but only if everything goes well, and everything never goes well. I am too tired, always, I have too little time, always, and it is not worth it. I wait a while and decide to sell. If only I never had.

My lifelong friend is on hand: let’s go to the shop together, you return it and swap it for something else (a SUP board or a bicycle? Fantasies, but meanwhile let’s go and then we will see). A week goes by, another goes by and nothing, we never find the time, the summer is ending and I leave for the holidays (loading it on the roof and making the trip at 110 an hour, I would not even think of it!). Then a surge of pride from the friend, who goes into sales-agent mode. “An old friend, someone like you, put on a bit of weight, he would like to start again, so I told him, he will call you…”. And hell begins.

I want to get rid of it, so I am accommodating - too accommodating - and I set a low price, giving up straight away on recovering what I spent (I have used it for only half an hour, one could at least try, no?): let us do it quickly, let us do it now!

“Listen, you know, I have a guru friend, he says the board is a bit old…”. He wants to haggle over the price, I think. That can be done, as long as it is short and intense. I am wrong: “Since tomorrow and the day after there will be a blow, if I can come by and pick it up to try…”. “As long as you give it back to me the way I gave it to you, OK?”. But it is a trap: our mutual friend points it out to me delicately: “Watch out he does not give it back to you in two years’ time”. I had not thought of that: and perhaps he will break something too, or make a great fuss and offer me a ridiculous price or… I decide to play for time and postpone the appointment by WhatsApp: “Tomorrow I can’t, I can’t manage to give you the gear”. Then when he comes back with the following day I am even more explicit: “My week has got messed up, I can’t manage to let you try it”. And he stops answering: damn, did he want to get in a couple of sessions at my expense and then, maybe yes, maybe no, give it all back at his leisure without coughing up a penny?

I have already pulled everything out of the garage, I take a few photos and meanwhile I also put it up for sale on subito.it. An aggressive price and unprofessional photos, but it is a site where the exchange happens in person and the idea is “here is what it is, if it might suit you then I will show you everything before we close the deal”. Another terrible idea (and to think I am usually sharp): the army of surfers arrives and I am not prepared. I do not know whether (they) are after the bargain of a lifetime (how much of a discount can you wring out of a 700-euro thing?), whether the game is to find the sucker to fleece or the desperate soul to bully, but as we chat the memories of youth begin to resurface, and it comes back to me why I stopped and above all why I have always been allergic to the official clubs: “I love surfing, I detest surfers” could be a good summary. “Hi, I’m interested but the price is too high”. All right, let’s talk, make me an offer. “No, I don’t want to be the one to make you an offer”. OK… I look at a few similar ads, all much higher than mine, and I think that 50/100 euros changes nobody’s life. I have a feeling, so I do not even try with -50 and go straight down by 100 euros (as long as it is short, and everyone is happy). And I am probably wrong: he does not want to make an offer, but he reacts vehemently and gives me an international finance expert’s analysis of all the details of every single piece, demanding more photos in order to be more precise (after those he will certainly pull again) and offers me half. Bye, face laughing with tears in its eyes, and I block him. I should have gone down by 10 euros and started the bazaar, but time is worth more. I pass. And another one arrives: “I would need more precise photos, above all of the third panel of the sail…” plus a series of questions all about the sail (there is the photo with all the specifications and he has not noticed? What can he be looking for in the offending panel?). Another, who must have all the photos at the best possible resolution, discourses on market trends, but does not make the offer because it has to be me who… And so on. All experts, all in need of data (images, specifications, technical elements, rigging procedures), all extremely well up on the jargon, and all with the same strategy: puff out the chest, belittle the value of the product, try to get a lower price (but by how much? What desperate man lets his neck be wrung over gear for fat guys nostalgic for the nineties?). And so, after three or four little lectures on how I should have presented the product (I am not Amazon, you know?) and on the price I should have set and on how to split it up and so on… I got back the urge for the sea, the urge to surf, the urge to stay far from surfers and their always identical stories of always identical nonsense, of mythical waves and wipe-outs seen, done, heard about at sixteen when… Oh well: perhaps it is the wind, the sun, the salt. I am keeping the board, I will try going out without the jacket that bothers me, and I hope there is wind on a day when I have time and am not tired. No time-wasters, in short. And no surfers.
