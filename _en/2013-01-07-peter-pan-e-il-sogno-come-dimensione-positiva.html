The capacity for imagination and for dreaming is the figure that measures the life (life more than vitality) of the characters and carries them into a dimension that lies even further beyond the mythical. The lost boys, who are they? It is almost horrifying to think about it in a realistic logic… Well, inside Peter’s story they are pure joy, pure life, the quintessence of vitality and of the capacity for imagination. Neverland is not the Land of Toys. It is neither a slump nor a wreck: it is adventure, virtue, strength, challenge, courage. Life! The moment and the point at which this island intertwines and intersects with real life is precisely the moment at which it loses any shadow of sadness or childishness. It is the moment at which it acquires a depth and a strength that are boundless and invincible. It is a place so fantastic and so amazing that it can give joy to the horror of the lost boys (never born?), so grand that it carries the capacity for adventure everywhere. Every child would set off in a second for Neverland. Every adult does not, because he no longer thinks it possible. Well, the moment at which this place intersects with the life of an adult, it acquires all the potential of hope. A particular hope, that is, the hope of adventure. The one that makes life gripping, that opens up to the extreme possible, the one that keeps us alive and open to the deep possibility contained in the real. The complete opposite of an escape from reality. It is a proud challenge to the depth of the real, to the invincible and inexorable desire of every man’s heart…

Dreaming, in my conception, is not an escape from reality. Quite the opposite: it is a perfect vision that challenges my heart to be alive, to ask for more and more, more and more until it is truly enough….
