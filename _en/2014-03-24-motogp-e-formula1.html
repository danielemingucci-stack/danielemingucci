After the first Formula 1 Grand Prix with the new rules, all hell broke loose with criticism: from the lack of noise (but on TV there is not much difference) to the over-complicated system of crossovers between electric motors, movable wings, tyres, traditional engines and so on. And, so as not to lose the good habit of tarring everything with the same brush, MotoGP was dragged in too. Only that the first MotoGP Grand Prix was great fun, hard-fought and full of twists, even though at least three or four leading riders were missing.
So the lesson is that you cannot tar everything with the same brush: last year’s Formula 1 was much more entertaining than MotoGP, where the three Spaniards lined up until the end of the race, while today the positions have been reversed. Formula 1 has taken the wrong direction and MotoGP the right one, at least judging by the first Grand Prix of the two disciplines.
The truth is that rules count, and a great deal, in all sports, and it is right to get to work on them and try to shuffle the cards when situations go stale. The rules, and the updating of the rules, their refinement, are the soul of sport: it is banal to say so, but without rules sport would be a brawl, or even war. The Greeks had rules even for battles between the poleis, in ancient times.
I think F1 has got in a bit of a muddle and has to work to sort things out, but I do not think it is a scandal: whoever takes a penalty risks missing it. They tried; now they have to fix it. MotoGP instead got it right, and we saw a motorbike race again after so many years of boredom. I think it is time that whitewashed monuments like FIFA too made up their minds to bring sports like football into our century and try to avoid championships with teams on almost maximum points…
