Between theory and practice there is a difference, as we know. And we know it dramatically, because putting an idea into practice always exposes you to a thousand unforeseen things that call it into question and, many times, even if it remains a good idea, make it fail. So theory, inevitably, always finds itself in a somewhat bad light: practical people above all think little of it, because they know that in talk it is simple, but then somebody has to solve the problems.

But there is also the opposite case, which is perhaps underestimated, and it is a high-powered bomb.

Because if preaching-well-and-practising-badly translates into chatter without achieving anything, making yourself look good with words and then weighing only your own advantage, practising badly and preaching-as-you-practise is even worse. Preaching badly means thinking badly, desiring badly, imagining badly. And yes, the trip in reverse - from practice to theory - loses a lot of pieces along the way. Perhaps in a self-interested way too (because when a theory justifies a practice, usually…).

And the idea of the tribe set against the family is a process of this kind. I mean a piece of nonsense, first of all. Having little imagination about what a family can be (it is a container that the thousand cultures of the world have filled in ten thousand different ways, which is to say that there is not only the bourgeois kind of family, Rondoni) offers no foundation (theoretical, moral, aesthetic, whatever you like) for setting it against a thing that is another thing: the tribe. Yes, for the dominant subjects the tribe does have a charm (Attila screwed them all, and he will certainly have enjoyed that), but the tribe, thank heaven, is not there in all cultures - avoided or overcome. Certainly tribe against family is a bit like setting pedals against wheels on a bicycle. It is nonsense….

The tribe is an explicit image, with no metaphor. A nightmare place from which we have freed ourselves - almost - through centuries of struggle and suffering. A place where balances of force and mutual control hold sway, dominant bloodlines that keep hegemonies generation after generation, fixed balances and never the chance to shuffle the cards, to redistribute tasks and opportunities. The thing furthest from the valuing of merit. Utterly disgusting. A rubbish idea. And a self-interested one.
