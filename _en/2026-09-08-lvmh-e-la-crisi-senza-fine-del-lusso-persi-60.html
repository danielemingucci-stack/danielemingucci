LVMH and the endless crisis of luxury: 60 million consumers lost, the middle class flees «aspirational» purchases

There is nothing left to say, no story to tell. It is urgent that we start again, because the more and more and more has had nothing inside it for far too long. Luxury is losing, it has no life left in it, no wonder, it is not unique, it is not special. It costs, it is snobbish, exclusive. It excludes, it pushes away, and when it does welcome, when it takes you in, it gives nothing (give?! Good God!), it adds nothing. It is accumulated, flaunted, inhabited, but what you inhabit is nothing, emptiness.

Life, stories, adventures, wonder. Hollywoodland, Bollywood (however it is spelled), the Côte d’Azur, the winters of St Petersburg, the intrigues of Vienna and Paris, Milan, Capri… the intricate, lucid stories of feeling, of passion, of suspense and suspended waiting. Taut hearts releasing beauty from the eyes, from the expressions, from the forms. All lost, clogged, fattened, industrialised (to be read syllable by syllable) and stuffed with torpid savoir faire… Well… The stories, the lives, the hearts, the passions, the gazes, the true search for destiny and salvation have wearily faded away, watered down in an endless dripping of dregs.

The stories told, the lives lived, the passionate hearts, the illuminated passions, the burning gazes, the suspended destinies must come back, as soon as possible.
