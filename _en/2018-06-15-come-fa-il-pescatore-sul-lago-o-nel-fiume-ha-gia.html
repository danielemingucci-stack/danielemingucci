As the fisherman does, on the lake or in the river. He already has two, three lines in the water and he waits. Good bait, good stretches, waters full of fish, but he is not catching anything yet. He prepares more; he does not take out those already in the water. Perhaps for a moment, to check that everything is in order. With one eye on the floats he moves a little and casts more lines. All done properly, all lined up, all with care. But the waters stay still: is it the wrong day, the wrong place? Or is it me, simply me who is wrong? Why is nothing biting, why does nothing happen? Certainly, every instant is one moment less of waiting. Every instant, by a little, I get closer to when it will bite. One instant after another, ever closer, closer, closer. But nothing. Nothing, for now. Another instant, and another.
I hate fishing, I hate waiting, however industrious and responsible it is. I hate its reasonable stillness, I hate time itself while nothing happens. Why, why not straight away? Why in a while, short or long as it may be? What fills that static, inert time?
I drown in those motionless waters, in the empty bobbing of brightly coloured floats. I drown while I wait for the action, while the image of that moment builds itself perfect in my mind, while the details take on colour and reveal themselves, unravelling one after another. But meanwhile everything is still, stolen from my losing dreams, dreams unable to happen, to take on the rhythm and the breath of this motionless reality. I drown in an emptiness that does not take my breath away, while the beats grow and rise towards my temples in waves of panic and rage. What is wrong? Is it perhaps, simply, me? As in a game of Risk with unlucky turns, I cannot accept even this: sooner or later luck too will turn; I only have to wait - alert and industrious - for that moment when it comes all of a sudden. I hate fishing; I will hate it even after I have caught something (perhaps this is the problem, but I am not able to get out of it).
