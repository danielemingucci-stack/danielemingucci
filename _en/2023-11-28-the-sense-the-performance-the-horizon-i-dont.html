I don’t believe we will be losers compared to AI, and the reason is the sense. But there is a transition to make because the “means,” the tools, are right in the middle. Yes, because AI is a truly high-performing tool, and evidently, for almost everyone, it is taken for granted that people should be too. Just as it seems taken for granted that everything should be connected and under the command of a network governed, precisely, by a formidable AI. Or that rules are inherently paralyzing, bureaucracy is a useless burden, and taxes are a necessary but odious obligation. Etcetera, etcetera. Commonplaces that help us move faster, a sort of excellent code for AI-style performances, but that actually disconnects us from the true function of things and reality, relationships, and ultimately our lives.

Function… does it work! Wins, performs: is that the only criterion? Rather, is it a satisfying criterion? In an accumulation perspective, certainly yes, but then let’s shift the question: is it truly enough for our hearts?

I think things can also be read in another way, by putting people at the center, one by one, questioning the deep function of what surrounds us, realizing that even excellent performances make sense “if.” It is as commonplace as the question of what purpose it serves to have a billion in the account the day before dying and especially the day after, if (or if not)…

Perhaps, by introducing something a dear friend taught me to call W.O.W., the why of why, a new light can infiltrate many obvious things that are not really obvious: absurdities like paying taxes being a right and an opportunity (to contribute to the common and shared good), that bureaucracy works poorly and weighs when not properly organized, but if well-structured, it would help, that rules, as in sports, are exactly what allows our talent to express itself and move forward (can you imagine Federer on a tennis court without a net and lines?). But, even in this case, rules written well, with criteria.

Just to say that the same fears of the great ones regarding AI, the letters from Harari and Musk, perhaps take for granted a horizon that we could very well refuse, that of efficiency and control as the sole “function” of existence. I believe we are neither masters nor servants, and that is not our destiny. We are humankind.
