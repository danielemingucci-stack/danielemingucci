Publisher wanted. Not exactly…

I remember Eugenio Scalfari treating Fabio Fazio almost like a naive boy when Fazio congratulated him on 50,000 copies sold: «Fifty thousand copies are nothing» if you want to have an effect on society… I put that together with the rare and courteous feedback I have received from illustrious publishers (I was about to write it with a capital, Publishers!) or from well-connected literary agents, certainly of a good level. And I do not find much difference from the feedback I have received from more or less expert friends, culminating in the very recent «I’m not some publishing pimp, you know», which I will perhaps understand in a thousand years. Was I offended? Of course. Every time. Because I would like to improve, I would like to learn, I would even like to understand, but I cannot find a road to do so. I like it, I do not like it, too much of this, too little of that (when it goes well), but zero practical pointers for reaching the public, and above all a latent censorship that I really do not understand: the story never seems to count for anything. Damn.

If I look at the Americans, Hollywood and all the mythology that revolves around it, having a good story is fantastic, hugely important. It is the base on which to begin a piece of work, to work out narrative strategies for example, to look for a tone of voice, to give face and personality to the characters, context, background and so on and so forth. And perhaps to make a team! Here, instead, what bounces back at me is «you are too much like Brizzi» (but who is he?!), «too much quoting» (but what is that?!), which give body to a mini literary essay devoted to my next flop. No lead on how to improve (surely being less like Brizzi and quoting less are not a road to success or to good writing?), not even a vague hint of what it takes to reach the public. Yes, the public, people, the ones who feed the billion-dollar film industry, the ones who convince Netflix in 2017 that investing 6 billion in productions is good business, those ones: are they not people who love stories? Is there or is there not a market for stories? I will cut it short and come to the publishing pimp. A noble banquet where all the ladies are old and the milords flat broke. An image that borders on sexism, except that sexism does not even exist in circles where the roles are immovable: the élite preens itself, quarter-hour after quarter-hour of isolated celebrity, it is far from everything, it looks for talent by competing over who is weirder or haughtier, and above all it deludes itself that I or anyone else is looking for a publisher. In Italy. The only one making money from books is Amazon; Hollywood makes money (and culture, yes, culture) hand over fist with stories that are good or not so very good. You people are no longer in the Middle Ages, you are in the Palaeolithic, and Italy, well… in Italy either you know someone or you find a pimp (that is what he meant, is it not?): what is certain is that you stay what you are, and a road for growing and learning, if one comes your way, is far more than a stroke of luck.
