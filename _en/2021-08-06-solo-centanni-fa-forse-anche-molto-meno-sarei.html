Only a hundred years ago, perhaps much less, I would have been missing a limb, much more than lame, with three balls :) and breathing problems with everything that hangs on behind them, from breathlessness to sleeping badly. And instead I am well, not perfectly well, but I play tennis, I swim, I go on bike rides and play with my children, I hug my wife and much more. Six or seven operations (do tonsils at the age of four count?), all successful, not all perfectly, nothing perfect, but in reality… my life has changed enormously. Even the time spent in hospital, what a difference from the first times! Better and better, both the manner and the effectiveness. Last time they told me at what time the pain would pass and… it may be suggestion… to the millimetre!

And I trust.

I trust commitment and ingenuity. I do not do it blindly: I have learned that with doctors you have to negotiate, that having your say, asking whether there are options, whether you can have more or not do something, are important things… then I trust.

It is a real nuisance, every time, when you are ill, to entrust yourself to imperfect treatments knowing that in a few years everything will be better and so on and so forth. But I am here now, I am a man of my time, and my time is much better than a hundred years ago. At my age my grandfather was old; I fly like a butterfly. A hundred years ago… Today, instead, those imperfect doctors, their assistants, the drugs, the machines fly forward one step after another and do incredible things.

I trust. I trust their science, the method they have… even if I negotiate, discuss, get my bearings, decide, if I do not trust I am nothing: I do not know everything, I understand nothing, and I would zigzag like an ignorant weathercock. A dear friend used to say it many years ago, that trusting is better. Yes, trusting is better, he said exactly that. He did not say to give your trust away to anyone, but trusting, being able to trust, is better! Because, for better or worse, we always trust something; even doubt or fear has to be trusted and… yes, even doubt and fear, so why not science, or those who slog from morning till night to treat us and to make every day in hospital perfect and so on and so forth.

When I trust, I hook on to someone, I find my bearings as far as I can and then - democratically - I enter into relation, I bind myself.

Whom am I attached to when I decide? Whom am I binding myself to, what am I defending, how much do I think I alone am worth, when I sit in judgement… Where am I going, and why do I think I am worth so much? Another friend was telling me that where she lives, on the other side of the world, people die with less agony, with more simplicity, as if they were going somewhere - but perhaps I am interpreting badly - and it were not the end of everything. As if there too they trusted someone.

Those who do not trust, who never throw themselves in and are never convinced, I understand little. I understand that they hurl at anything at all their own fear, their inability to control and to be certain of something, their inability to be in their own time, and that they preserve with almost ferocious energy a something they do not in fact hold in their hands. Or perhaps, but these are fewer, they get excited when they have a tiny bit of power and use it to shout no to something, anything that everyone says, and they instead no, they are more, they understand more and are stronger than everyone… They trust fear and understand nothing, because doubt and fear muddle the few ideas they might have in their heads and block any likelihood of knowledge, of friendship, of community…

So I would like to write a letter to put a different idea on the table, given that I do not trust the way so many tell it and let themselves be taken by doubts and fears that disorient, darken and dishearten everything and everyone. A letter to ask ourselves whom or what we know how to trust, and to decide together how to play our power, looking ahead. Yes, looking ahead while we ask ourselves what we are sparing or preserving ourselves for. Because perhaps we think everything will be fine and that this good will simply happen, leaving everything as it is. But that does not seem to me an idea, a solution, because Covid is here, we have almost died of hardship and so on and so forth. I am not saying, in short, whom to trust, or to trust whom I trust, but when we decide whom, questions like what is it for, where does it lead, will it be enough… those we can all ask ourselves.
