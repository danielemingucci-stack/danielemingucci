At the linguistic level - of the history of the language but also of the history of culture - is not the centrifugal motion away from Latin, the positive charge towards making oneself understandable, listenable, communicable, an important trait? The freedom with which words adapt to the line is spectacular. The multiplicity of forms of the same word offers the chance to shape the line perfectly.

In reality the process runs the other way. The vernacular is born from this expressive freedom, from the will to adapt (to make adequate!) the meaning to the best form of communication (is communication not the meaning, the sense of the words?). The language frees itself and at last expresses itself in a new splendour. It bends down to the person, to the listener, and it flowers. It is born without betraying the origin, the etymon, but this etymon becomes richness of expression, of form.

A new flowering of form is born that enhances, but at the same time overflows, the embankments of the Latin tradition. It is literally the flowering of a root. The language is invented, found…
