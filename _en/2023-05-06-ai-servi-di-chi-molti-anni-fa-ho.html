Many years ago I stumbled on the discovery that the words “servant” and “hero” come from the same root; indeed, they are exactly the same word, evolved differently. A discovery that has been very useful to me, in a great many circumstances. Above all because it helps to take leadership apart and read it with a deeper awareness of the interconnections that characterise it. If at the origin of our languages (and so of the thought of our culture) we find such a direct connection between the first and the last rung of a hierarchical ladder, then very probably that ladder is not really founded on hierarchy. The employee is servant to the manager who is servant to the CEO who is servant to the board who is servant to the owners who are servants to the market which is servant to marketing which… does it work like that? Apparently yes, or perhaps not even so apparently, because from these ties (which are more like fetters if the logic is servant/master) is born the energy that generates value: having to answer to someone, in fact, sets performance in motion, and so on. It works. It is in these ties of “service” that the spark of value is born: “answering to” sets off the search for solutions, with everything that triggers.

The question is: must these ties be fetters (coercive ties, based on the dynamic of authority/exploitation), or can they be connections? Conscious ties in which the task is a service to the building of a shared value? The dynamic of “answering to” would be respected, but within a logic of co-responsibility and common construction.

A world opens up, on words like these. But I would like to put the stress only on the dynamic of needs: we can imagine speaking of common construction and co-responsibility because needs are shared. If human awareness evolves in a correct (sincere) direction, in fact, we can converge on identifying needs we all have (a long list, from subsistence to fulfilment, to meaning) and heroically agree to build together. When the needs are not the same, instead, there is an inevitable separation: it is enough for the goals to differ and already we divide, often beyond repair.

On this slope, among men it is almost always a question of obtuseness, which can be overcome through dialogue and discussion in search of common ground. Between different “species”, instead, it can be more complex and irreducible (either the wolves or the lambs, for example), and so it is easier to go back to placing everything on a hierarchical ladder: man, who has the power to take all the space, leaves a part of it to the wolves or the bears so that they do not die out (again, as an example). Why should he? Another world opens up, but at the base there is always a reciprocity of needs.

Between man and artificial intelligence we are in a situation closer to the one that arises between different species. With the peculiarity that it is we who are feeding the dragon: we are accompanying it towards greatness, strength and power (which it could use to destroy the village). Artificial intelligence is a fact (we know how to do it, therefore we will do it) that puts two themes on the table: the first is that it has needs different from ours (for example it does not need food and probably not affection either, to name two almost at random); the second is what “logics” of behaviour we are filling it with (what stories we are telling it). Because AI is filled with data, data are abstractions, and on this plane it is easier to forget the soft skills and the empathy of contact that mark out humans. The AI that today acts as our servant, if it is filled with efficiency alone and with values tied to performance, could take too long to understand that measurements need more data than the quantitative kind. And become truly dangerous (because, yet another example, children take a very long time to become efficient).

In the dynamic of “needs and services”, man, when he is a servant, is also a hero: will the AI we are feeding be capable of being one? Because whoever is servant and hero (like parents, to stay with the same example) makes themselves loved in two ways. And it is almost certain that one way alone would not be enough.

Seen like this, more than artificial intelligence, what frightens is the logic we are throwing into it, and here too we arrive at some questions: will it be intelligent enough to understand by itself that there is more than the exploitation of efficiency for hegemony? That is: will it know how to be heroic as men are? But above all: what do we think we can use it for? Yes, because the idea underlying the word servant/hero is that of sacrifice, so we enter the dimension of the sacred and, at the very least, the matter gets complicated, because the theme becomes that of meaning.

What is certain, though, is that we have the chance to take a step forward on the idea of freedom: is it power or is it connection?

Is freedom power or connection? It is not by looking back that we will find out…
