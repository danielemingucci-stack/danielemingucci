A provocation, but not that much of one. The subject is innovation, product innovation. The dynamic seems carved in stone, at least seen from Italy. Here is the process. You have the idea and you start studying it. You look at the market, the competitors, the opportunities, and meanwhile you file away at it in your head, you refine, you optimise. And of course you start working out how to produce it. With whom. It all comes rather naturally, because while you are getting the details clear you also bring in people who know the material, people who make things. Of course, again, you go to someone who is good and who is precious for understanding better, for refining further. And - with a little gratitude for what they have taught you - the moment comes when you entrust them with production, all of it or part of it.

Let us change point of view. Let us see it from the side of the craftsman, the maker. You are good, you know your trade. You earn, but you earn by working. You have a return on reputation and on the market, but it is a return that is hard to see, because all it means is that you are never short of work and that you get paid for it. It is not easy money, it is money you sweat for, one euro on top of another. And along comes the innovator, with his idea. Something you need a little - it might be the turning point - but that your business does not need at all. Because it means other headaches… entertaining ones, but troublesome. Yes, the challenge rekindles old emotions, and out of the drawers of memory come ideas that had lain buried under too many things to do… temptation runs its course. And off you go.

Another change of view. Here we are! Idea validated, team ready to go, financial and economic burdens under control. It really begins. And you go in medias res, you collaborate, you exchange thoughts, you study solutions, you cross skills and visions. It is an exhilarating moment, from many points of view, a moment of creation, of invention, of innovation. But it is also the moment when nobody sees the problems, and the key mistake has already been made. A quote, an estimate of time and costs, but no contract. Is a quote a contract? Yes and no, because there are no guaranteed times and methods, no penalties, no terms of sale. How could it be otherwise? There are too many unknowns, too many details nobody knows: it is innovation, nothing is really codified. And the craftsman, on top of that, is a wild animal anyway: he knows what to do, but he does not plan, he moves by instinct, one phase at a time, he sees, reasons, acts… A rough cost, a rough timescale, then talent, skill, craft.

But this is where everything will collapse, this is where everything jams every time. Because it is never as easy as it looks, and the unexpected does not come only from inside, it comes from outside too. An important job, a client you cannot say no to, an impasse that needs thinking through… the time stretches, the costs stretch, the startup gets into difficulty with its investors. And so on and so forth. From here on there are thousands of shades, depending also on the human quality of the relationship, but the stop is inevitable. And damaging. But what is the damage? How can anyone imagine charging it to (and making it be paid by) the damned, disorganised, wild craftsman? He would never sign a contract with a penalty - nobody would sign one, in truth - but even if he did: what can be the size, in millions, in billions, of a startup that is never born? And so on and so forth.

Ideas for getting out of it? Perhaps, but before ideas we have to admit that this is how things go. That the wonderful, superhuman, incredible craftsmen - with no irony whatsoever, because they really do possess a priceless heritage of practical knowledge - are not companies, are not real economic entities, do not have certified organisations or the capacity to answer for their actions, not once off the beaten track. And they are not reliable, not at all. The damned craftsmen.

PS

I am - in fact - a craftsman.
