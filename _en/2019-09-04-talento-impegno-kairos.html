Beautiful things should cost little. A few polemical examples? It is easier to become champion of the neighbourhood cross-country race by training three hours every day, or to have a beautiful house by spending dizzying sums, to become little tennis champions with the coach three times a week, and so on. The same product - a car, a dress - at one price is beautiful, at another it is so-so, is it not? A €100 T-shirt, say…

This is not praise of the cheap, but of kairòs: reaching results at an excessive price (in commitment too) may not be worth it. It is not. What is beautiful, what is adequate, right, good, what is OK… must be within reach, must fit into a general context, must have a proportionate accessibility. Yes, it is an inclusive way of thinking, perhaps too much so, but the tension is towards measuring the true value of things, without rigging the cards. Because then talent should be spent on matches that truly deserve the commitment: becoming world champion of something niche niche niche proves what? Not talent, objectively… will, perhaps, but that wears out.

Beautiful things should cost little, and commitment should always be put in, but all of one’s commitment should go only into things that deserve it. Getting results through super-commitment is rigging the cards: if a Chinese worker works twenty-two hours a day he will of course produce more than a European, but does he have a life? Is that an adequate proportion? Is it really worth more, or is he simply destroying himself to win on a single parameter? Talent, humanity, kairòs… Living well should cost little, and the greatest efforts should be made for things that deserve them.
