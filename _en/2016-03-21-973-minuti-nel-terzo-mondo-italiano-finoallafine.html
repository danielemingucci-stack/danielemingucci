Another great record broken in this Golden Age of Italian Football. A goalkeeper’s unbeaten run (“the greatest of all time!”, they trumpet to the four winds) is getting ever closer to 1,000 minutes! It is the same goalkeeper who, midweek, lets in 4, the most important of them in the 90th minute… 15, 16, 17, nobody can count any more the consecutive wins strung together by steamroller Juve, and the run of consecutive league titles today gives no sign of an end. Winning, winning, winning: the only thing that counts! For two World Cups running, though, Italy has gone out in the first round (it had happened only with the famous Korea match in the ’60s, right?), but that is soon forgotten at the start of the new season. In Europe last year the miracle was almost touched, but it would have been a miracle, because Italy’s level in the Champions League is the round of sixteen, no further. Winning, winning is the only thing that counts, and the Italian system asks for results and asks for them at once, or rather always, according to a successful model, the Juve system, precisely.The most promising coaches, then, interpret as excellent professionals this diktat of the market (we fans are the market, and above us the directors who pass on the people’s “gut” and set the executive line for the coaches). And the coaches all become the same: Garcia is no longer Garcia after a few years, Sousa will do the same, and so on and so on and so on. Everything lines up and “lowers itself” to the only thing that counts: winning. Results are demanded, not play. It seems a contradiction or a nuance, but it is not, and the proof is that the Italian football system has never been this low. We are slow, we are predictable, we have a single team that can compete in Europe; behind it there is a desert of provincial sides that take turns claiming their quarter-hour of fame but cannot express more than that.
There, I am anti-Juventus, and precisely for this reason. The emotional intensity with which the whole Juve establishment pursues the goal of winning winning winning has created this footballing third world that we are living in and will live in for many years in Italy. It is a situation declared explicitly: winning “is” the Juve style. When style should instead be a way in which the result - winning or not winning - is obtained. Nuances and word games? I believe they are details that make the difference and that little by little take away, or will take away, interest even from those who today enjoy these thrilling winning streaks as fans: a foregone result in the long run bores even the most frustrated (how many Juventus coaches have left at the peak of their performance simply looking for “something else”, the Champions League, the national team…).
Seba Rossi’s run was made during a championship in which there was competition; today it is a dyed-in-the-wool Juventus man who is the first to say: “when I played, the match against Juve was the match of the year, today instead…”. Winning, winning, winning is a “style” (but that word should not be here, by God!) that has brought Italian football to its lowest point and keeps it there.
The press, the fans, the directors must ask for play, not results. Results, in Italy, belong to Juve; that is not what one should compete on! One should play football, enjoy good play, let innovative teams grow, create styles of play, smile at the thrashings and use them to risk more, not to seize up (see Garcia). But it is also true that, with good play, results ought to come: in Italy unfortunately it is not so, thanks to a system of little helps, bookings or non-bookings (blatant penalties are used less; indeed, opponents’ statistics are kept high with piles of penalties in matches already won, there is a certain refinement), of newspapers and broadcasts under guard, besides the risk of sudden and devastating crises (as soon as a Garcia, a Mancini or whoever gets close in the table and gets a little cocky, the so-called wrath of God happens, inevitable and devastating, have you noticed?).
The intensity and motivation with which the Nazis fought the war was their indisputable strong point (it is an example, hey!) and there was no competition, nobody was at that level. That system was worn down and defeated only thanks to allies who brought different values into play, from which they were able to draw an unstoppable strength that did not simply “win”, but created a world different from the efficient order we were destined for. Winning is not the only thing that counts; winning is good, but playing is even better. Rage, fury, those things have nothing to do with sport, and it is time to start asking something else of our poor football. The results themselves, after all, speak clearly… and this is an extremely entertaining paradox: find me a Juventus fan who would not give the last four league titles in exchange for a Champions League. Many would give a star as well!
Something else is needed; fans and press above all, it is time they made themselves heard.
