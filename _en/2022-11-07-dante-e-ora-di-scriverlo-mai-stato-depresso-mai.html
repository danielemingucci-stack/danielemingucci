Never depressed, never had a crisis. He ran up against his own limits, yes, like all those who dare and put themselves to the test. He challenged himself with an intensity that certainly put him under strain, but he had only one thing in his head: «to say». Dicer di lei quello che mai fue detto d'alcuna, to say of her what was never said of any woman, was never either a fixation or a duty. He is not a censor, Dante, he is not severe, not even with himself. It is that after what he had seen he could not stop himself looking for the most compelling and adequate way to tell it.

Ineffability is a constant theme: you cannot manage to say what you live. There is no way! Emotions get expressed, that is simple, but life: how do you imprison life, how do you set it free with words? It is an intuition, and so it is an enthusiasm that stirred him all his life: finding the best way, the most adequate (adaequatio intellectus ad rem, which is the definition of truth) to express what one has seen. It is as if a gratitude had forced him to go ever higher, to pit life and talent against an impossible undertaking: saying the ineffable… It is a vertigo to which he applied himself diligently, at first. He wrote ever more perfect rhymes, he challenged the best of his age and of the past, he entered into the language, the metre, the images. He climbed ever higher, ever higher, towards perfect Rime. So perfect, so adequate to the height he was heading for, that they became… Petrose, stony, incomprehensible. Difficult. Far from everyone’s life, too elevated, as the mosaics of Ravenna, so marvellous and perfect, raise God to a royalty so high as to be far from any possible life. No, that was not the road.

He had to “say”, he had to show; what he had discovered and held in his heart was too beautiful. His poetic father, Guinizzelli, had given him a spark so precious that he felt called to set all of life on fire, everyone’s life. So he does the second thing we would all do: he explains. He takes his talent, all the best of his art, and writes the Convivio. Poetry, the highest poetry, and philosophy, the highest art of all, to guide his readers towards knowledge. Poetry is the only form adequate to the heights that fill his heart, the only way of showing respect for the wonder that has filled his life. But it is difficult, it is not immediate, and so prose is needed, explaining is needed, which is reading between the folds. It is like augmenting the text, taking the lines one by one and revealing their secrets, switching on a light over the depths that animate them and justifying their sense. The Convivio is a marvel, the greatest work of literature up to that point, true perfection. But for Dante it is not enough. Depression leads you to destroy everything, not to finish, to leave things incomplete. You magnify your limit, you shout the frustration of your imperfect nature at destiny and you smash, break, bury. But for Dante that is really not the issue. He has too great a debt of wonder towards destiny and life. A life which, among other things, never stops putting spokes in his wheels and tangles in his way. It is always like that when you try to do something great. But perhaps it is also the secret of true greatness: to draw on misfortunes and find even more energy, to shout the beautiful with even more life. And of that Dante truly lacked nothing. Let us try to put ourselves in his place and forget all the more fanciful reconstructions of his frustration: can you imagine telling of Paradise merely out of a wish to be crowned poet? Clearly that is not enough. Let us come back to earth: you are writing an incredible work that is turning out very well and is at last coming close to expressing, to making clear, the secret that sends through your poetry an energy never seen before and… And you change road. Yes, because not even that is enough (Dante is not a perfectionist, he is simply sincere). Seeing is not only understanding. Understanding is important, but it is still not enough. Seeing is like a journey, life is a journey. When you are in the dark of a wood it is not that you are sad or depressed, it is that you see nothing, you do not understand where you are, you cannot find a direction, you remain at the mercy of what happens, wild beasts or whatever you like, and you can go neither forward nor back. Even climbing a little hill becomes a feat. Dante knows it, he identifies with it to the full, he knows that “not” seeing is like this, and he understands that when someone is in the dark it is not enough to tell him there is light further on. You have to go and fetch him.

He leaves the Convivio not even half done (what a marvel, the Convivio) and comes to fetch us. Yes, many talk about his boundless ego, about his having wanted to be a character too, and so on. I ask the same question: can a boundless ego tell you about paradise? Dante makes the journey together with us, in the same boat, with the same fears and the same limits. He takes us by the hand and carries us with him. First he goes down to the bottom of that dark, the dark of the wood. Then he overturns everything and begins to climb. A journey, at last, is adequate to tell us of that wonder. A journey of encounters where, man to man, limits and truths are seen (he is not a censor, he is not one who saves and condemns, Dante. He meets, looks, sees and walks). We walk, we climb, we climb towards that wonder. Whole, complex, testing the limits of every heart and daring them not to stop (stopping is not enough, that is obvious in the Inferno). The supreme poet makes himself anything but distant; he is so free as to leave everything and agree to make a journey with me. To go down, when the heart would rather go straight up, and then slowly to climb the mountain. One step at a time, into the mystery of the heart and towards the wonder of truth. Or of beauty. Or of justice… Saint Augustine is worth listening to on this.

Free, certainly, invincible, attentive, passionate, determined, sure. His poetic father, Guinizzelli again, says that Al cor gentil rempaira SEMPRE Amore, to the gentle heart Love ALWAYS returns. Always! Everything begins with a certainty, with an encounter that, in a way you cannot put into words, makes you certain of an “always” adequate to your heart. This is the gift Dante gave us. To stay sincere with that wonder which, behind Beatrice’s eyes, let him see a truth so great, for himself and for everyone, as to “force” him to be sincere with his own heart and to accept the incredible journey that life set before him.

He stayed faithful one step at a time, he burned his energies and his talent right to the end without ever ceasing to shine, not even in the midst of the bitterest difficulties. Perhaps he did not even see them, the difficulties, as he sailed in that wonder. What else did he need, after all, if with this truth everything shone?
