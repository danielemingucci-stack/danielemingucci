“To one he gave five talents, to another two, to another one…”.

There is a unit of measure. None of them broke them into pieces. One, two, five. Nobody did half over here and a third over there and the rest for something else. The reckoning is in wholes.
A man had found a piece of gold, and he had been told that by searching in that place he would certainly always find more. But it was not prudent to trust too much to fortune: it was more proper to put it to use, to make it yield. He was certain that this was his talent and he was determined not to bury it. He divided it and then divided it again and then divided it once more. One grain he invested here, another there, and so on and so forth. He did the same thing many times and scattered his gold dust. But dust is dust, and it was swept away. He forgot to search where he had found it, and waited for his investment to yield. “I had friends with whom I invested my first portion, then I had others with whom I invested more and more and more”. But he had not given everything to anyone and, to this day, no investment had yet come back. His little powder was not magic.
“What I lived with you I have lived with others too”, “There is what must be done, and there is what must be done in order to do it”. We move to action: having pocketed the nest egg, the talent I meant, it is put to use. There are no rules, or even if there are they come long after the value of that precious talent. Because that talent is a gift, but it is much more: it is an election. And it is a responsibility surely given to reward and make the most of abilities. A census must be taken of who has received them, and things must be organised well. Dividing fairly, evaluating carefully and acting accordingly. Sharing the judgement on everything is fundamental, so that nothing is wasted or traded in vain. In short, things must be done properly. United we will do better than alone. We will form an association and each of those who has put in something of their own will be rewarded in proportion: this too makes everything safer.
Only a few held out and went on risking, and they were looked at as madmen, as irresponsible: you keep going back to that field to look for more gold! And what if you find no more? What if it runs out? You could break it up and share it with far more intelligence, you could trust your friends more and judge together how to make use of it! You are irresponsible. And self-referential.
Most were certain: one has to deserve one’s talents, and it was inexplicable that so many were granted, and continually, to adventurers of that sort. But as we know, God’s mercy is strange and infinite. Certainly, though, it is best to keep away and not let oneself be influenced or tempted. To God the things of God.
So, year after year the ditch became ever deeper. The field full of gold was less and less visited, or was visited with shame and caution: being there meant having gambled away one’s own gold, having failed in a certain sense, and that was not a good thing. The responsible ones went there rarely and almost in secret, only to remember: they knew very well how to make gold yield, and they certainly had no need of a stroke of luck to find more.
And God’s measure? Why are pieces found and not gold dust? Why does one have to keep on searching for it? Why does one have to stay attached to him? When will the fruit of all this toil be gathered? When will the field at last be empty, and commitment, responsibility, be rewarded?
