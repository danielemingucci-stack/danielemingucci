What would school be like with mobile phones switched on? No, because even the most open among us, the most advanced and the most “wifi for everyone”, at the idea of having phones on during, say, a class test… would perhaps set a limit.
In real life, though, it is not quite like that: I can use my phone to check something, to look at a figure, an image, in practically every situation, so… the simulation of the class test is perhaps not preparing for anything real.
These are disorderly thoughts, but this idea is beginning to intrigue me. One thing that is certain is that today’s teaching staff and syllabuses are probably not able to withstand the impact of pimply kids connected to who knows what site or what chat, but let us try to imagine what it would be to discover “together” the history of Rome, the sources, the works, the battles, using to the full what the net can offer us! Let us try to imagine the difference from the little lesson learned properly, the book studied by heart and so on. I do not know, it intrigues me a good deal.
It also intrigues me to think of the system of relationships that can be built by “sharing” during a class test. It used to be copying, but today it is sharing: anyone who does creative work knows that, but not only them; think of what “rulings” are for lawyers or accountants: you look at what others have done, you take inspiration, you copy…
I realise that it is dangerous, perhaps, that it is all still to be built, but reimagining the student-teacher and student-student dialectic with a tablet or a phone in hand is starting to intrigue me more and more. Hungry and foolish… searching and mad: that could be the thing to propose to the new teachers: be mad, and make our kids hungry!
