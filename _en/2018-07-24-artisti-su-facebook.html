The photographer gets irritable if he cannot exhibit his art; the artist applauds him because “it” must scratch. And our pliable faces must bow to the high demands of expression, to approval, to likes. On Facebook.

The dossier or the story of an evening is raised to a work of art in a place that is a non-place, where we can be consumed, liked and linked, approved of or perhaps mocked, face to face but behind our backs. And we must bow to the flair and the temperament, to the sensibility of the artist. On Facebook.

And the long university conversations come to mind, on aesthetics and the places of art, on provocations, conceptual ones, mind, not of transgression, about bottle racks and bidets (plenty of bottles get drained at a party, plenty of bidets get polished, even more). The places of art lay claim to their own virtue, and we, instead, are there. On Facebook.

Yes, because for all the libertines and the provocateurs - artists or not - gaining access to the places of art is an exercise in relationship. Those who do not manage it set themselves up as little virgins - I haven’t prostituted myself, not me - but the truth is that the beautiful is relationship, joining, inspiration. It is a relationship that has to be accepted, both in the creative phase and in the phase of exhibition; it is a direct, tangible contact that forces you to grow and to change, perhaps with pretence but above all with steady nerves, the heart well connected to the brain. And eyes wide open. It is a relationship that accepts what “is not right” and, one or the other, either explains it and convinces, or changes and becomes something else, surpasses itself. It takes calm to react, it takes control to shoot, it takes patience to be good (artists). Those who do not accept the relationship accept no limits, and those who do not are playing - with other people’s faces. On Facebook.
