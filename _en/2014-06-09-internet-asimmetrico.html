The timing of the internet is not exactly, exactly “symmetrical”. In general you find everything on the internet; there is a way to get several versions and even several visions of the same piece of information. All in all, if you set your mind to it, in most cases there is also a good chance of tracing things back to the sources to get reliable data.
In general.
In “local” detail, instead, you often get blackouts even of weeks. Who won the elections in such-and-such a small town, who got onto the town council and who did not? It is only an example tied to the last elections, where, searching online, nothing or almost nothing can be found out about many town administrations even 15 days later. Or, again as an example, about checking the CVs of many candidates…
There is an asymmetry, in short, if we think instead of what a wealth of detail and particulars can be found, and in real time, for events even very far away. The point is that on the internet somebody has to put the information there, and where digital culture is less widespread the timing is that of the old press… even worse.
Perhaps it is a banal concept, but many practical examples show that the “coverage” of events is by no means guaranteed, just as the quality, precision and timeliness of the information are not guaranteed. What can one say, then? What is new about the internet lies in the simplicity with which data and information can be put into circulation. What is old lies in the fact that somebody has to want to do it and has to want to do it well. In the press there is a register of journalists and an editorial responsibility that gives some guarantee of quality; there is the commercial interest of the titles in being timely and offering quality. On the internet, instead, we are all on the same plane, and the plane might be tilted. Or cracked…
