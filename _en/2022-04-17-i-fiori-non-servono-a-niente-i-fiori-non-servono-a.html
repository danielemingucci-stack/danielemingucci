Flowers are no use at all, but my garden is full of nothing else. I am sorry not to be useful to others, but I spend my time above all watering them, tending them and admiring them while I dream with their colours and their thousand scents. I hope some expert botanist can prove me wrong, but it seems they do not grow and do not become anything: all they know is how to die and be born again, morning after morning. If you pick them they dry up at once, and then nobody knows what to do with them.

And precisely for this reason, everyone’s looks tell me clearly that the time I spend gazing at them and dreaming of new ones will all be lost, like the little talent I have left. Only that every time it is stronger than I am. Watching them bud and slowly bloom has within it a mystery and a wonder that carry off all my senses, my heart and my brain too. So strong is the habit of losing myself in that irresponsible amazement that I fear I no longer know how to do anything else. And yet I admit it, I recognise it, and I accept the comfort of those who explain to me that the fault is neither mine nor anyone’s: they are no use at all, and for this reason it is wise not to let oneself be carried away, and to devote oneself to something else…

In the end, though, it is a comfort I keep at a distance; more and more often I avoid those who sternly shake their heads when they look at my useless pride and my senseless joy. My lawn is a scandal in the sun from which, most are by now certain, nothing will come but scent and buzzing. But it is full of flowers and my heart is flooded with cheer, my eyes shine with colours and I can hardly breathe for all the scents. Of what will be born tomorrow I have an uncertain vision, but every today will be beautiful like this, bright with a thousand smiles and with wonder. It is those flowers that bring it, and it is a magic we can never afford to miss.
