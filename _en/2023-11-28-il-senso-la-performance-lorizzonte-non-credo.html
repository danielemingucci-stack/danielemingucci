I do not believe we will be the losers against AI, and the reason is meaning. But there is a step to take, because in between there are precisely the “means”, the tools. Yes, because AI is a truly high-performing tool, and evidently almost everyone takes it for granted that people should be too. Just as it seems taken for granted that everything must be connected and at the orders of a network governed, precisely, by a formidable AI. Or that rules are paralysing in themselves, that bureaucracy is a useless weight and taxes a necessary but hateful burden. And so on and so forth. Commonplaces that help us go faster, a kind of code that is excellent for AI-style performance, but that in reality disconnects us from the true function of things and of reality, of relationships and, in the end, of our lives.

Function… it functions! It wins, it performs: is that the only criterion? Or rather, is it a satisfying criterion? From the point of view of accumulation, certainly yes, but then let us move the question: is it really enough for our heart?

I think things can also be read another way, by putting people at the centre, one by one, by asking about the deep function of what we have around us, by realising that even excellent performances make sense “if”. Which is as banal as the question of what use it is to have a billion in the bank the day before you die and above all the day after, if (or if not)…

Perhaps, by bringing into play something a dear friend taught me to call W.O.W., the why of why, a new light can slip in upon many obvious things that are not so obvious at all: absurdities such as that paying taxes is a right and an opportunity (that of contributing to the common, shared good), that bureaucracy works badly and weighs on us when it is not properly organised, but that if it were well structured it would help, that rules, as in sport, are exactly what allows our talent to express itself and run ahead (can you imagine Federer on a tennis court with no net and no lines?). But, in this case too, rules written well, with good sense.

Only to say that the very fears of the great about AI, the letter of the Hararis and the Musks, perhaps take for granted a horizon that we could perfectly well not accept, that of efficiency and control as the only “function” of existence. I believe we are neither gods nor servants, that this is not our destiny. We are humankind.
