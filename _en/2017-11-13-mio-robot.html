On the coming of robots we have to get in first and concern ourselves with the distribution of wealth before imbalances, social injustices, tragedies arise. Being afraid of them is the first instinct, but we know very well that certain developments cannot be stopped through prohibition, which, contrary to what one would wish, could create imbalances of competitiveness that would be a cure worse than the disease. No, we have to ride this technological opportunity while avoiding the creation of monopolies and dominant positions. The technological possibilities and the culture - the humus - exist today; let us not wait until we are caught unprepared!

How? Well, robots need to be a shared heritage. The robot that does the work (or part of the work) I used to do should be mine and not the company’s. I should be able to benefit from its productivity in the same measure as the company. It should work for me too, in short, not only for the “boss”. Is it a stupid idea? Let us dig out Marxism again, distributism, the protection of the middle class and that of the working class, if we like, but the point must be the sharing and the fair distribution of this technological wealth. We have to grow together, everyone has to grow; development and opportunities have to be participated in, for everyone. And everyone has to work at building the right interaction between man and robot.
Other scenarios, in whatever key one tries to read them, cannot help seeming apocalyptic to me. Artificial intelligences that replace ours, perfect drones that monitor and distribute anything at all, from products to security, getting to the point of being able to reproduce and improve themselves, are frightening not only in a scenario of conflict; they are frightening in a prospect of harmonious peace too, because they would take fantasy and imagination away from every development. No, we have to keep them close to us and we have to “use” them, enjoy them, own them. They must be a common good and a good of each person; it is the only way for fantasy not to die together with justice and hope.
