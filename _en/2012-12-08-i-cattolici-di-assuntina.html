Morresi is always partisan, incapable of dialogue and even of a quarrel. She has her idea in her head and goes straight on, flattening everything and everyone. She weeps for the Catholics, but Vendola and Bindi will not do for her. And so on (if only it were just them!). Above all she thinks to impose by law or by politics an aesthetic as well as a culture, unable to notice the world she is immersed in. Are they all wrong, Assuntina? Perhaps they are, but in that case they are to be corrected little by little. She (she herself!) passes and flattens. She will become a church hen - if she is not one already. And to think I liked her so much.

(Sent to Il Foglio)
