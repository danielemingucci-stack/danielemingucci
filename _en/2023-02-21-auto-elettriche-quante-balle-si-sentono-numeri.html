You hear figures that make no sense on earth or in heaven, more or less in every newspaper. Let me give a few others, real ones and not presumed.

With diesel at around 1 euro 50, before the war and the price jumps, doing 100 km took about 10 euros. That was the reference figure; then there was the time you got away with less and the time it went worse, but that is the money that comes out of our pockets. Then there are the insurance costs, the road tax and - this is important - maintenance. As scheduled as you like, but always rather expensive. With long-term leasing, all these costs are “hidden” inside the monthly fee, but they are there all the same.

Now, for a moment, let us reason from the point of view opposite to the consumer’s: people are used to putting their hands in their pockets to spend this money, so why should we stop taking it from them?

Let us come to the electric car. Insurance more or less the same, no road tax in many regions and - hear ye, hear ye - no maintenance! Or at least, Tesla, which has only ever made electric cars and has no network of workshops to keep going, has no maintenance programmes. Indeed, what is there to maintain in an electric car? The brakes are hardly ever used, there are no filters and no top-ups… there is no gearbox, no distributor, no turbo, no hose clips, no intercooler and not even the thingamajig… So we can put a zero. Not quite: going back to what I said before, from the point of view of those who have always taken money under the heading “maintenance”, the idea of putting a zero against one of the most important items in the accounts is not nice at all. So to the electric project many brands add planned obsolescences of various kinds that they could do without.

Finally, consumption. Let us remember the figure: more or less ten euros to do a hundred kilometres is what we have always given them. I will stay with Tesla: with a 70 kWh battery you do 400 km very comfortably. Here the costs are wildly variable in the statistics you read in the papers, going up to charging points that sell at 0.99 per kilowatt hour. In reality, when you have an electric car you take out some subscription or other and practically never pay more than 0.40. At home, then, you are at around €0.20 per kWh. Let us do the homework: at home a full charge comes to 14 euros, which means 100 km for 3.50 euros. At the Tesla supercharger (where you never do a full charge! But we will talk about that separately) it is 42 euros, which brings us to the fabled 10 euros per 100 kilometres (Elon is no saint either). Those are the numbers and, with maintenance and road tax at zero, the costs “per year” are not bad at all. But… if you are a car manufacturer, with a fine press office and a business model that has made you money hand over fist, if the market is used to putting its hands in its pockets and spending that money, what would you do? Would you talk about the future, or would you give a little push to those who tell tall tales and adjust the numbers?

On the table there is no “handing over of leadership” to foreign countries, no costs weighing on workers’ shoulders, and not even the loss of millions of jobs (how many charging points will have to be built and installed in the coming years?). There is the possibility and the task of drawing the future, perhaps with quieter roads, less blackened by smog. And if you really cannot give up the roar of engines, you can always go vroom vroom while you drive, can you not? :)
