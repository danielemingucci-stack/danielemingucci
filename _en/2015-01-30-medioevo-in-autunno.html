When Guido Guinizzelli wrote Al cor gentile, around the year thirteen hundred, it is as if he had lifted a veil, the veil that covered the silent crisis of a civilisation that had become static, stale, oppressive. He caught the breaking point of a perfect doctrine which, in reality, was anything but.

The Middle Ages, Catholic civilisation, had gone into crisis because of one small detail: the nobles. Not nobility, but the nobles. “Gentil per sclatta torno…”, gentle by lineage: the nobles, with their rights and their privileges, had wiped out all the content and all the freedom of Christian doctrine, which - precisely - could no longer be anything but doctrine. Perfect, beautiful, grandiose, but crushed by a status quo in which everything was forced to be … still. There were rights, there were privileges, there were dominant positions, and nothing could be touched.
Abuses of power, many, but above all a poison that had pervaded everyone’s minds and hearts: at a certain point one stops even thinking - let alone desiring - that one could change position, place in society. With all that goes with it.

Christianity, if it crashes, always crashes there, on privileges…

A few weeks ago the head of my son’s nursery school called me in: “the board of directors has told us that if we do not find twenty-five new enrolments, next year we close”. Why do we have to find them? Why does a board of directors, from on high, hand down this instruction? On the board of directors are the children and relatives of the founders: they administer and dictate the line, but then who finds the customers? Exactly…

That is how things end; it ended like that once before, around the year thirteen hundred: with the valour of a whole army, the captain of the day took the fief for himself. And kept it. And passed it to his son, who did not even know how his dad had obtained it. And he administered it and dictated the line.
Merit that becomes privilege, privilege that becomes inheritance… only now do I notice the perfect rhyme, in Italian, of eredità with aridità, inheritance with aridity…
It ended like that once before, when the cry of freedom probably threw the baby out too with the bathwater. Yes, the baby… that baby is my son: will he have a free school in a few years? Will he have a summer festival? Will he have places in which to grow his own ideals? What will they be?

I am calm: al cor gentile rempaira sempre amore, to the gentle heart love always returns. Always: too beautiful.
