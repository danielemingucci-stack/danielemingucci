I have short experience of dealings with care communities and with rehabilitation organisations in general, but in all the cases I have been able to come across - except for those who cynically manage public funds specifically set aside - there is one condition of entry: “he” has to ask for it.

Yes, but he does not want to. He does not want to, does not want to, does not want to! And then there is no remedy; without the will there is no remedy.

“His” will is engaged elsewhere; there are all the surrounding psychic dynamics, the perfect plans hatched so that nothing ever succeeds and the pathological destructive mania adds defeats to its own hunger… and the most obvious gap always remains that of desire. A small warm nest, insecure but welcoming, replaces the natural infinite boundary that every human gaze desires. A thousand barriers come between fear and courage, a thousand castles are built, a thousand starvations wear down and mutilate the will and the capacity to get back up. Life is consumed in this and… Somebody do something, for heaven’s sake!

Yes, because, in the end, the illness one carries is precisely that of not wanting!

But if he does not want to, we “can do nothing”. They are like those products people used for giving up smoking: “do not smoke during the treatment”, the instructions said. No effective approach can be found other than this one (which does not work, because it presupposes that the sick person behaves as cured at the start of the cure). It is reality, only reality can force someone to ask for help, to be sincere, to set things in motion. Then strength, in company, can be multiplied, directed, but the first gesture is necessarily that of a man, strong or weak, healthy or ill, capable or powerless before reality, before things. Apparently there is no condition in which this power, this responsibility, can be delegated to others. And I do not know what to do, I do not know what to say while dear friends destroy themselves… it is not all right with me, in short, it cannot be enough, it even smells of self-absolution in some respects, and something escapes me.
