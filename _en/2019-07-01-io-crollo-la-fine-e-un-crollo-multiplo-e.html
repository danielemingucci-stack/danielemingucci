The end is a collapse, multiple and articulated. No use hiding it by trying to build a progression. Not least because the point is not the ending: it is the chain of events that makes one curious.

It arrived one winter evening thanks to an instinctive choice and a special trip to the DHL depot: the parcel is out of standard and delivery is less efficient than usual. Then the lad who helped me load it explained that with one more day of patience it would have arrived at home, but… the weekend was upon us, I detest that stiff, untameable sofa, so I got in first and went to fetch it so as to assemble it that same evening. Hard to pass judgement: at first sight you hope it is better than something from Ikea, but to begin with it has to be said that the instructions are less complete than their standards. Which is not enough to stop me: a couple of traps avoided (there are no facts, only interpretations), the armchair shows off its colours and shapes in front of the stove. It is a wonderful year.

Spike, blessed enthusiastic little dog, christened it as home after a few minutes, but he knows his place, and when the master is sitting he does not protest and at most comes over for a cuddle.

Stove sofa armchair facing TV PlayStation computer, with curtains and windows behind. It is the panorama of our very long winter, a time-lapse of life and hearth, somersaults and sleeps (watchful sleeps in front of the Play or real sleeps in the warmth of the fire) of this wonderful year.

And summer comes, in a day, because at the end of May the stove was sometimes still going, then suddenly a sultriness worthy of the Belgian Congo. All the balances change, the symmetries, the compositions: the stove counts for nothing, the TV little more, and the Play… well, the Play is like the soul (quadammodo omnia and, in any case, a subject apart). A few months, and the armchair has changed look and function several times, with an almost insurmountable crisis when Spike ate part of the padding and then fell in love with it. For a few weeks, with the complicity of the heat, the unlit stove and my lack of interest, it was the dog’s armchair and… that could well have been the end. But no! A surge of pride: covers off, washed, put back in shape, it resumed its place, an aesthetic, defended by a nice blanket that will stop the dog doing damage, decorated too by the sofa cushions, which are not needed in summer. It will not be like the winter, but a wonderful summer is in prospect.

It is the details, as in a detective story, that hide the snare or the clue. The PlayStation has vanished into the basement den, with a master stroke, and the ménage of the living room has very different rites, though the arrangement stays the same. The armchair is mine even if in the heat I do not watch TV, and the stove, obviously, is out. Spike suffers from the sultriness and in turn frequents it less, bothered too by the cushions that make the approach harder, the leap to get on and lie down. The detail, precisely, perhaps devised by the perfidious cleaning lady who calls him “tsittsione”, fatty, in a threatening tone, even though the little scamp has a body fat index that would make a triathlete turn pale. A wonderful dog.

I have to look something up on the Internet and I decide to integrate myself into the Sunday asset, which involves a low-slung-underpants-and-mobile-phone contest on the sofa. I am dressed in T-shirt and shorts, but I sprawl like the head of the family (I do the same thing they do, but they are messing about, I create). Armchair, cushions, I shift it slightly so as not to be in the way and I sit down with the phone already in my hand. I have not said that the multicoloured armchair is very particular: an ingenious mechanism can turn it into a bed, a chaise-longue (I looked it up on Google) or, precisely, an armchair. It has two wide legs that keep it balanced, plus another four that pull out when needed. I sit down. There are the cushions: better! On something soft I will be even more comfortable. Laura is late; I try to take it calmly, pretending to myself that I am relaxed. I integrate with the mobile-phone gang (but I create, I study, I do not mess about) and I lean my back properly, the way I like. And I fall backwards. The armchair, damn it, is almost against the stove, but not enough to lean on it and save me. I desperately stretch my legs forward, find a glimmer of balance and for a fraction of a second I have the chance to take control. But «help» is the fatal cry. Where the fuck is Laura, she has been gone more than an hour. I collapse. Onto something soft but without mercy, without hope, without dignity, in an alienating fraction of a second: I have to look at something on the internet, I sit down for a moment, what (fffk) is complicated about that? What can there be that is so unforeseen unforeseeable banal as this?! I collapse. Fuck. Legs vertical towards the ceiling lamp, between the stove - against which I am pushing hard with my head, but only for better balance - and the compact mass of seat and cushions, vertical and parallel to my legs. Help! Come and help me, actually no, no, do not pull me up, phone your mother! On my side, to the left, towards the sofa is much better. I tuck my legs to my chest, turn on myself and get into balance for the… heave with which - but is it possible she is still not back, Laura? - I will get back on my feet. Feet down, one hand on the armchair, the other on the arm of the sofa, and up. The children are obedient; perhaps they are laughing, surely they are thinking up idiotic jokes, out loud too, but they immediately abandoned the idea of lifting me bodily by levering on armchair and parquet… and I, much more nimbly than I thought, am on my legs and heave: on my feet! A wonderful leap (Boom!).

What the fuck! Is Mum answering or not? It is the window! What the fuck has the window got to do with it now?! I have just fallen on my back, armchair and all: why is the window sash flying towards the ceiling, off its hinges? And Laura…? All right, she has nothing to do with it and obviously it is not her fault, but Andrea is almost choking with laughter as he gets through: «Mum, come, Dad is destroying the house», and he laughs fit to burst. While he gets the line I get the window, in mid-air and incredulous: I sent it flying with my shoulder as I stood up, but it is saved, thanks to the sofa too. An imperceptible hint of a curse crosses my lips but dies at birth; the window is intact, Laura has answered, I have not hurt myself. Mattia disappears into his room holding back his laughter, Andrea offers me help, fearful of getting involved in further accidents, Samuele tells me how Grandad does it when it happens to him (is that possible? Is there a case history of flying windows?).

I breathed deeply, I did not swear, I did not have to count any damage, but I recognised clearly the touch of the flow of life asserting its own absolute, all-embracing, complex control. The power of one who knows how to create cannon shots, not just impacts or big crashes… It is clear that the mystery speaks to you looking you in the eye, sends clear signs, links up personalised plans: if the armchair is not enough, here is the window… But I can be lightning-quick to understand (and in my heart I have to admit that the armchair would not have been enough): OK, I am calm, everything is OK. Murphy is dead and Laura is on her way.
